The Market Story

East Palo Alto is moving fast right now. There is only about two months of inventory, and well-priced homes are closing in roughly three weeks, often over asking. Of the 80 single-family homes that sold in the last year, the median closed at $1,085,000, around $831 per square foot, in 21 days.

For a home like 2247 Menalto, the right comparison is the cluster of 1,000 to 1,150 square foot homes. Those sold from the low $900,000s to about $1,200,000, with a median of $957,500 and about $889 per square foot. The higher numbers in that group, like 2512 Farrington at $1,325,000, are renovated or on better lots. The honest comparison for a 1948 home that needs the buyer to see past its condition is the $905,000 to $1,000,000 band, which is where most of the size-matched sales landed.

The single most important fact in this file is the home's own recent history. It was listed in January at $1,098,000 and withdrawn in May without selling. That is real, property-specific data: the market tested this exact home at that price and said no. The lesson is not that the home lacks value, it is that the entry price was too high. In a market where a sharp price creates competition and an ambitious price creates silence, the path forward is a confident reset, not a repeat.

The unpermitted rear unit changes who the buyer is. For an owner who wants rental income or space for family, that unit is worth real money, even unpermitted. We market it honestly as a bonus, disclose its status up front, and let the buyers who value it compete. That is what carries the result toward the top of the range.

Reading the numbers

Of the 80 East Palo Alto homes that sold in the last 12 months:

  • 24 homes (30%) priced it to sell and sold over their original ask, a median of $1,095,000, about 5.4% over, in a median of just 10 days.
  • 13 homes (16%) priced at market and sold right at their asking price, in about 12 days.
  • 43 homes (54%) overpriced and sold below their original ask. They started the highest, at a median list of $1,150,000, but sold the lowest, a median of $1,080,000, about 4.7% under, and it took them 35 days, more than three times as long.
  • 20 homes (25%) had to cut their price at least once, a median cut of $79,000, before they found a buyer.

Why so many under their ask? It is not a weak market. Priced-right homes still sell in days and over asking. It is a pricing-discipline gap. Most sellers anchor to the optimistic number, a top neighbor's sale, a Zestimate, or what they want to net, and the market corrects them down over weeks. The disciplined minority who price at or just under true value set off competition and get bid up. 2247 Menalto already sat on the wrong side of this at $1,098,000.

What this all means for 2247 Menalto. The market punishes optimism and rewards a sharp price. The home tested the optimistic side at $1,098,000 and did not sell. The fix is to reset sharp, in the high $900,000s, so it reads as the deal of the block, draws the widest buyer pool, and lets the rear unit and competition push the final number up. That is the entire case for the recommendation below.
