2366 Glen Way is a 3-bedroom, 2-bath single-family home on a 5,000 sq ft lot in central East Palo Alto. The property was acquired in 2012 and has been held as a rental with reliable, on-time tenants. Wen is weighing two paths: sell vacant after the tenants vacate (retail buyer pool) or sell as-is with the tenants in place (investor buyer pool). The two paths produce meaningfully different price ranges and days-on-market outcomes — this report quantifies both.
THE MARKET STORY

Looking at the last 12 months, 3-bedroom homes near Glen Way have sold between $905,000 and $1,325,000. That's a $400K range on properties with the same bed/bath count and similar lot sizes. The difference between the top and the bottom of that range comes down to condition and how the home is sold.

At the top, you've got fully updated, vacant homes. 2512 Farrington Way closed at $1,325,000 in February 2026 ($1,205/sqft). 1523 Ursula Way closed at $1,220,000 in December 2025 ($1,109/sqft). These homes show well and let a retail buyer write a clean offer without picturing a remodel. The buyer pool here is wide: first-time owner-occupants, families coming over from Palo Alto and Menlo Park, anyone using conventional financing.

At the bottom sits 2547 Hazelwood Way. It sold for $905,000 in September 2025 at $823/sqft. Hazelwood is the comp that matters most for your situation. The property listed in May 2025 at $999,000, went pending, fell out of contract, dropped to $950,000, went pending again, and finally closed at $905,000 about 100 days later. That arc, from original list to final sold, is what an as-is or tenant-occupied sale tends to look like once the buyer pool shrinks to investors. It's the closest thing we have to a preview of how 2366 Glen Way would sell with tenants in place.

Here's the honest read on the tenant question: investors will pay for income, but they discount for hassle. Your reliable tenants are a positive. The property is producing day one. No make-ready, no vacancy lag. But the investor also inherits the rent (which under AB 1482 and EPA rent stabilization can't be reset to market on day one), the lease term, and the need to schedule showings around the tenants. That means longer DOM and fewer offers. In California, the typical tenant-occupied sale runs 5 to 15 percent below the equivalent vacant sale. If your tenants are paying well below market, you're on the wider end. If they're at or above market, narrower.

One legal note worth covering. EPA considered an Opportunity to Purchase Act in 2023 that would have required offering the home to tenants first. The city council voted it down. As of today, there's no OPA in East Palo Alto, so you have no obligation to offer the property to your tenants before listing. The city said it may bring the policy back at some point. Worth keeping an eye on, but it doesn't change anything for a sale you start now.

So the punchline: if 2366 Glen Way sells vacant and presents reasonably well, you should land around $1.05M to $1.15M and close in 15 to 30 days. If you sell occupied to an investor, $880K to $980K is realistic and the close takes 60 to 100+ days. The gap is roughly $100K to $200K. That's the cost of convenience, and it's your call which side of it you want to be on.

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS

East Palo Alto OPA (Opportunity to Purchase Act) — CURRENTLY NOT IN FORCE

EPA considered an OPA in 2023 that would have given tenants the right of first refusal on any landlord sale. The city council voted it down. As of today, there's no OPA in EPA, and you have no obligation to offer the property to your tenants before listing. The city said it may revisit the policy. Worth watching, but it doesn't change anything for a sale you start now.

Tenant Protections — AB 1482 + EPA Rent Stabilization

AB 1482 caps annual rent increases at 5% + CPI (max 10%) on most properties and requires just-cause for eviction after 12 months of tenancy. EPA layers its own rent stabilization on top. So whoever buys this property can't reset the rent to market on day one. They inherit whatever the tenants are paying now. If the rent is well under market, the investor's yield drops and their discount goes up. If the rent is at or near market, the discount shrinks.

Tenant Move-Out (if pursuing vacant sale) — Relocation Assistance Required

If you sell vacant and the tenants don't leave on their own, a no-fault termination under AB 1482 typically requires one month of rent as relocation assistance (or a rent waiver), plus 60 days of written notice for tenancies of 12+ months. EPA may add its own requirements. Budget $3-5K and 60-90 days before the home can be delivered vacant. That timeline still pencils out well vs the occupied scenario, but it needs to be planned, not improvised.

Property Condition Verification Needed

This report leans on public data (Redfin, county records, MLS public history). The 2012 listing described the property as "well maintained, central heating." Fourteen years of tenant occupancy can add up in deferred maintenance: kitchens, baths, flooring, roof, HVAC, sewer lateral. Before pricing, an interior walkthrough plus a basic pre-inspection (sewer scope, roof, foundation) would tighten the ranges above by $30-50K in either direction.

Information Gaps — Need from Wen

Three pieces of info would sharpen these scenarios. Current monthly rent sets the cap rate and the investor's discount math. Lease term remaining matters because a long fixed lease widens the investor's discount. And current condition: has the home been updated since 2012, or is it mostly unchanged? Plug those three in and the recommended ranges should narrow by 30-40%.
