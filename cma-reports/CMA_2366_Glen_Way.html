YEAR BUILT
2366 Glen Way is a 3-bedroom, 2-bath single-family home on a 5,000 sq ft lot in central East Palo Alto. The property was acquired in 2012 and has been held as a rental with reliable, on-time tenants. Wen is weighing two paths: sell vacant after the tenants vacate (retail buyer pool) or sell as-is with the tenants in place (investor buyer pool). The two paths produce meaningfully different price ranges and days-on-market outcomes — this report quantifies both.
THE MARKET STORY

East Palo Alto in mid-2026 is a market with real depth and real spread. In the last 12 months, comparable 3-bedroom homes within a mile of Glen Way have sold anywhere from $905,000 to $1,325,000 — a range of more than $400K on properties with nearly identical bed/bath counts and lot sizes. That spread is the story. What separates a $905K sale from a $1.3M sale in this neighborhood is almost entirely condition and how the property is brought to market.

The top of the market is being defined by fully updated, vacant, move-in-ready homes. 2512 Farrington Way closed at $1,325,000 in February 2026 ($1,205 per square foot), and 1523 Ursula Way closed at $1,220,000 in December 2025 ($1,109 per square foot). These are properties that show well, photograph well, and let a retail buyer walk in and write a clean offer without imagining a renovation. The buyer pool for this tier is large — first-time owner-occupants, families relocating from Palo Alto and Menlo Park looking for value, and conventional financing.

The bottom of the market — and this is the most important comp for Wen's situation — is being defined by 2547 Hazelwood Way, which sold for $905,000 in September 2025 at just $823 per square foot. The property was originally listed at $999,000 in May 2025, went pending, fell out, was reduced to $950,000, went pending again, and finally closed at $905,000 after roughly 100 days on market. That trajectory — original list to final sold — is the textbook pattern for an as-is or occupied sale where the buyer pool narrows to investors and value-add operators. It is also the closest analog we have for what 2366 Glen Way looks like if it's sold with tenants in place.

The truth on the tenant-in-place question is this: investors will pay for income, but they discount for hassle. A reliable tenant who pays on time is a positive — it means the property is producing day one, no make-ready, no vacancy lag. But the investor is also pricing in the things they can't change: they inherit the rent amount (which under California AB 1482 and East Palo Alto rent stabilization can't simply be reset to market), they inherit the lease term, and they coordinate showings around a tenant's schedule, which means longer time on market and fewer offers. Across California, the typical discount on a tenant-occupied sale runs 5 to 15 percent below comparable vacant sales. Properties where the tenant is paying significantly below market can see steeper discounts; properties where the rent is at or above market see narrower ones.

There's one piece of legal news that's relevant and recent. East Palo Alto considered an Opportunity to Purchase Act (OPA) in 2023 that would have given tenants a right of first refusal when a landlord listed the property. The city council rejected it. As of May 2026, there is no OPA in force in East Palo Alto, so Wen has no legal obligation to offer the property to her tenants before bringing it to market. The city has said it may revisit the policy — this is worth watching, but it does not affect a sale started today.

The bottom line: if 2366 Glen Way sells vacant and shows reasonably well, it should land in the $1.05M to $1.15M range in 15 to 30 days on market. If it sells with tenants in place to an investor, the realistic range is $880K to $980K, and the time to a closed sale is likely 60 to 100+ days. The gap between those two outcomes is between $100,000 and $200,000 — that is the cost of convenience, and Wen should decide which side of it she wants to be on with eyes open.

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS

East Palo Alto OPA (Opportunity to Purchase Act) — CURRENTLY NOT IN FORCE

East Palo Alto considered an OPA in 2023 that would have given tenants a right of first refusal on any landlord sale. The city council rejected it in late 2023. As of May 2026, there is no OPA in EPA and Wen has no legal obligation to offer the property to her tenants first. The city has indicated it may revisit the topic — worth monitoring, but not a constraint on a sale started today.

Tenant Protections — AB 1482 + EPA Rent Stabilization

California AB 1482 caps annual rent increases at 5% + CPI (max 10%) on most properties and requires just-cause for eviction after 12 months of tenancy. East Palo Alto also has its own rent stabilization ordinance. This means the future investor-buyer cannot reset the rent to market on day one — they inherit the existing rent. If the tenants are paying significantly below market, that compresses the investor's yield and increases the discount they'll expect. If the rent is at or near market, the discount narrows.

Tenant Move-Out (if pursuing vacant sale) — Relocation Assistance Required

If Wen decides to sell vacant and the tenants do not voluntarily vacate, owner move-in or "no fault" termination under AB 1482 typically requires one month of rent as relocation assistance (or rent waiver), plus 60 days written notice for tenancies of 12+ months. East Palo Alto may impose additional requirements. Budget $3-5K for relocation assistance and 60-90 days of timeline before the property can be delivered vacant. This timeline still nets out favorably vs the occupied scenario, but it needs to be planned.

Property Condition Verification Needed

This report uses publicly available data (Redfin, county records, MLS public history). The 2012 listing described the property as "well maintained, central heating." Fourteen years of tenant occupancy may have introduced deferred maintenance — kitchens, baths, flooring, roof, HVAC, sewer lateral. Before pricing, an interior walkthrough and basic pre-inspection (sewer scope, roof, foundation) would tighten the ranges above by $30-50K in either direction.

Information Gaps — Need from Wen

Three data points would meaningfully sharpen these scenarios: (1) current monthly rent — benchmarks the cap rate and investor discount; (2) lease term remaining — longer fixed lease = bigger investor discount; (3) current property condition — updated since 2012 or unchanged. With those three inputs, the recommended ranges narrow by approximately 30-40%.
