Your Three List-Price Strategies, And What Each Has Done Here

Generally you have three ways to price a unit like this. The useful part is that your own building has run all three experiments for us over the last 18 months, so we are not guessing. Here is what each strategy actually produced, in your building, with real numbers.

Strategy 1: Price To Compete (my recommendation)
List ~$559,000
Set the price a few percent under the expected value to pull in multiple buyers and drive an offer date. In your building, #115, #230, and #310 all priced this way.

What it did here: sold over asking, in 6 to 19 days.
Strategy 2: Price At Market
List ~$579,000
Price right at the estimated value. Fair and clean, but it leaves less room for a bidding dynamic to carry the number higher.

Likely result: sells at or a little over ask, in roughly three to five weeks.
Strategy 3: Price Aspirational
List ~$599,000+
Reach for a top number and hope a buyer meets it. In your building, #322 and #332 both tried this.

What it did here: #322 is still unsold after 45-plus days, and #332 sat eight months, then sold for $525,000, about 12 percent under where it started.

The units that sold fast and over asking were not lucky. They were prepped, staged, photographed properly, and launched with a strategy. Here is the plan I would run for #214:

Prep (about one week). Light refresh where it pays for itself, deep clean, touch-up paint, decluttering. Nothing over-invested. I will tell you honestly what moves the needle and what does not, so you are not spending money you will not get back.
2
Professional staging. A 1-bedroom shows dramatically better staged, and every strong sale in your building was staged or professionally furnished. It makes the creek view and the floor plan read as a home, not a box.
3
Full media package. Professional photography including twilight shots of the creek side, a floor plan, and a 3D walk-through tour so out-of-area and cash buyers can tour before they fly or drive in.
4
Maximum syndication. MLS plus Zillow, Redfin, Realtor.com, and the full network, a dedicated property page, a direct email push to my buyer-agent database and the East Palo Alto buyer pool, plus paid social targeting.
5
Strategic launch with an offer date. List, hold a broker tour and first-weekend open houses, then set an offer date roughly 10 to 12 days out. This concentrates buyer attention and is what produces the over-asking result.
Expected timeline: about one week of prep, then roughly 10 to 12 days on market to the offer date, then a 21 to 30 day close. Call it five to six weeks from go to close, faster if we take a strong early offer.
