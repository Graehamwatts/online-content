Your Instinct Is Right. This Is a Tease Price
The $699,000 list price is not a market price, it is a starting line. Across 71 comparable homes that sold in this zip code over the last 12 months, the median sale was $1,075,000, and not one single home this size sold anywhere near $699,000. Even the roughest fixers in the area cleared $810,000 to $880,000, and an updated home on this same stretch of Senter Road sold for $1,050,000 in October. This is a deliberate strategy to generate maximum foot traffic and force a competitive offer date. One important nuance the ask hides in both directions: this home fronts a busy arterial, which is why it will not reach the $1,150,000 to $1,360,000 that similar homes on quiet interior streets fetched.

Where the Market Is. South San Jose 95111

Over the last 12 months, 71 comparable single family homes (3 to 5 bed, roughly 1,050 to 1,600 square feet) closed in 95111. The median sale was $1,075,000, about $856 per square foot, but most of those homes sit on quiet interior streets, so the raw median overstates what a Senter Road frontage supports. The market here also moves fast: median days on market was 12, and more than half of all sales went pending inside two weeks.

The single most important comp is 4136 Senter Road, because it sits on the same busy street as this home. Senter Road is a major arterial, and homes fronting it trade at a real discount to identical homes on the quiet interior streets. 4136 Senter was a tastefully updated home, remodeled kitchen and both baths, dual-pane windows, copper plumbing, on a larger 7,700 square foot lot, and it sold in October for $1,050,000 ($841 per square foot) with just 2 offers. That is the honest anchor for what a nice home on this street commands.

For contrast, the identical 1,308 square foot floor plan on a quiet interior street, 404 El Cajon Drive, sold for $1,150,000, and the renovated tier on interior streets ran $1,235,000 to $1,360,000. The gap between those numbers and the $1,050,000 same-street sale is the street discount, roughly 8 to 10 percent, and it applies to this home too. This home's renovation is deeper than 4136 Senter's (new heat-pump HVAC, updated electrical and plumbing throughout, and a fourth bedroom), which supports a modest premium over that sale, but the smaller lot (5,600 vs 7,700 square feet) takes some of that back.

Three Paths Forward. Offer Strategy

Anchor your thinking to the identical-model sale at $1,150,000 and the renovated tier above it, not to the $699,000 ask. Three paths, ranked by aggressiveness:

Path A. Value anchor. Compete only if the field is thin.

Offer range: $875,000 to $950,000. Below the cohort's original-condition sales. It only wins if the offer date draws a weak field, which is unlikely, a $699,000 ask on a renovated home will pull heavy attention.

Risk: ordinary original-condition homes in this zip cleared $950,000 to $1,000,000 all year, so this range means expecting to be outbid on a renovated home.

Path B. Competitive market-aligned offer. (recommended)

Offer range: $950,000 to $1,010,000. Anchored on the $1,050,000 same-street sale, adjusted down for that home's meaningfully larger lot (7,700 vs 5,600 square feet), its three-car driveway, and its park-like yard, advantages this home does not have. The top of the range credits this home's deeper renovation and fourth bedroom. This respects the busy-street discount instead of pretending the home sits on a quiet cul-de-sac.

This is the recommendation. It is grounded in the one sale that shares this home's biggest pricing factor, the street itself, with honest adjustments for what made that sale stronger.

Path C. Stretch offer. Lock it down.

Offer range: $1,010,000 to $1,075,000. This brackets the $1,050,000 same-street sale and moves toward, but stays under, interior-street pricing. High certainty of winning against most fields.

Risk: an appraiser anchoring on the same-street sale may still land below the top of this range, so plan an appraisal gap if you go here. And be honest with yourself about resale: the street discount will still be there when you sell.

Notes & Caveats

The busy street is the biggest factor in this valuation

Senter Road is a major arterial, and that is why this analysis anchors on the $1,050,000 same-street sale instead of the $1,150,000 to $1,360,000 that similar homes fetched on quiet interior streets. The roughly 8 to 10 percent street discount is permanent: it will apply when you buy, and it will apply again when you eventually sell. The flip side is that it also makes the home attainable, you get top-tier renovated condition at a price the interior streets cannot offer.

Why the gap between list and value is this extreme

A $699,000 ask against a realistic value around $1.05M is roughly a third under market, deeper than the usual Bay Area tease pricing. The likely goal is maximum exposure: at $699,000 the listing surfaces in search filters for a much larger buyer pool, packs the open houses, and stokes a bidding frenzy. Do not read the low ask as a signal something is wrong with the home. Read the disclosure package for that, it posts July 11.

The renovation cuts both ways

The full renovation, including updated electrical, plumbing, and a new heat-pump HVAC, removes most of the big-ticket risk that usually comes with a 1959 home, and that is genuinely worth paying for. Just confirm in the disclosure package whether the work was permitted. Unpermitted renovation work would change both the value math and the risk picture.

Appraisal risk

If you offer above roughly $1,050,000, expect the appraisal to be the friction point. Appraisers will see the same-street sale at $1,050,000 and the cohort median at $1,075,000, and they will apply the same street adjustment this report does. Plan an appraisal gap and talk the structure through with your lender before the 15th.
