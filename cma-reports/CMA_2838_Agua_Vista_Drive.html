Read the Listing Carefully. This Is a Structured Bid
Confirmed: this is a structured offer process, and interest is already strong. The listing agent has 12 disclosure packages out as of this writing, just days into the listing, and offers are due Wednesday, July 15. That is a real, early signal of a competitive field, not just a guess based on comps. The two closest comparable sales in this exact pocket both listed around $1,899,000 and closed 13 to 15 percent over that, at $2,150,000 and $2,190,000, through the same kind of structured process. If this listing follows the same pattern, expect a genuinely competitive offer date.

Key process notes from the listing:

  • Submit offers directly to the listing agent, Neil Le. CAR contract preferred.
  • Full disclosure package required with the offer: signed disclosures, proof of funds, pre-approval letter.
  • 3% earnest money deposit into escrow within 1 day of acceptance, a meaningfully fast EMD timeline that signals the seller wants certainty.
  • Offer date: Wednesday, July 15. All offers due by then. With 12 disclosure packages already out, plan on this being a real multiple-offer field, not a soft deadline.

There is also a nearby comp to be aware of, though it is not quite a twin: 2846 Agua Vista Drive, same street, same bed/bath/sqft (4 bed / 3 bath, 2,360 sqft, built 1972), is active right now at $1,700,000, listed 3 days before this one. It is explicitly marketed as a fixer-upper, "bring your tool belt," with no cooling system at all and an older shake roof. This home has window and wall unit cooling and a newer metal roof, a genuine step up in condition, which is consistent with its $45,000 higher ask.

Directly From the Listing Agent

Graeham spoke with the listing agent, Neil Le, directly. A few things worth knowing that shape how to read this listing:

  • The seller originally wanted to list higher, around $2.1M, using the recent 2854 Agua Vista sale (listed $1,899,000, sold $2,190,000) as the reference point. Neil talked her out of it, his read was that the home needs a little TLC and would not support that number out of the gate.
  • The $1,745,000 list price was set specifically to be competitive with 2846 Agua Vista's $1,700,000 fixer listing, priced a notch above it to reflect this home's better condition. Neil expects 2846 to also sell above its ask.
  • Preemptive offers are unlikely to be accepted here, even a strong one. Plan around the July 15 offer date rather than trying to jump the line early. Use the time before then to get fully prepared: inspection, financing, and disclosure review all done in advance so you can submit your strongest offer right at the deadline.
What a "strong" offer looks like here: given the seller's and agent's stated preferences, a strong offer combines a price in the recommended range below with the fastest, cleanest terms available to you, full 3% EMD into escrow within 1 day as requested, a pre-offer inspection so you can waive or shorten the contingency, a clean pre-approval or proof of funds with appraisal gap coverage, and a flexible close date. Price alone is unlikely to be the deciding factor if another offer matches it with cleaner terms.
Where the Market Is. Berryessa Right Now

Of 38 comparable Berryessa sales, 3 to 5 bedroom, 2,000 to 2,700 sqft, over the last 12 months, the median closed at $1,999,444, about $900 per square foot. Applied to this home's 2,360 sqft, that points to roughly $2,123,000, well above the $1,745,000 list. Narrowed to just the 14 closest 4-bedroom comps in the same size band, the median is $2,100,000, about $914 per square foot, implying approximately $2,156,000 on this home's footprint.

The two most directly relevant sales are 1638 Peachwood Drive and 2854 Agua Vista Drive. Peachwood is essentially the same tract-built home as this one: same year built (1972), same 2,360 sqft, same 4 bed / 3 bath count, same ground-floor bedroom and bath layout, walking distance to the same park. It sold for $2,150,000 ($911/sqft) against a $1,899,000 ask in January 2026, a 13.2% jump, through a structured multiple-offer process. 2854 Agua Vista, on the same street as this listing, sold for $2,190,000 ($1,052/sqft) against a $1,899,000 ask in March 2026, 15.3% over, also through a competitive offer process.

Both of those sales were renovated or updated (Peachwood: updated kitchen with stainless appliances, central A/C, solar. Agua Vista: full renovation with vaulted ceilings, new flooring, granite counters, central A/C). This listing has window and wall unit cooling only, and nothing in the remarks suggests a kitchen or systems update. That is a real difference, and it means this home likely will not command the same $2.1M to $2.2M level a fully updated version would. But it also means the fair comparison is not "what did the renovated homes get," it is "what would a home like this get if the same underpricing-to-drive-competition pattern plays out here."

Three Paths Forward. Offer Strategy

With 12 disclosure packages already out and a confirmed offer date of Wednesday, July 15, this is not a hypothetical multiple-offer scenario, it is a real one. Combined with the underpricing pattern in this exact pocket, three paths, ranked by aggressiveness:

Path A. Anchor offer with strong terms. Compete on terms more than price.

Offer range: $1,780,000 to $1,850,000. A modest premium to list. Win by being the cleanest, fastest, most certain offer: strong pre-approval or all cash, fast EMD (matching the seller's requested 1-day timeline), short inspection window.

Risk: if this listing follows the same script as Peachwood and Agua Vista, a modest premium like this likely will not win. Those two comps cleared 13 to 15 percent over their asks.

Path B. Competitive market-aligned offer. (recommended)

Offer range: $1,900,000 to $1,975,000. This mirrors the 10 to 13 percent over-ask pattern the two closest comps actually showed, without assuming this unrenovated home commands full renovated-comp pricing. It is grounded in what actually happened on this exact street and tract, not the broader median.

This is the recommendation. It respects the demonstrated demand in this micro-market while not paying for updates this home does not have.

Path C. Stretch offer. Maximum certainty of winning.

Offer range: $2,000,000 to $2,075,000. Near or just under the fully renovated ceiling ($2,150,000 to $2,190,000). Adds an appraisal gap, waives most contingencies after disclosure review, fastest possible close.

Risk: this is priced close to what fully updated homes fetched. For an unrenovated home, this range carries real appraisal risk and may mean paying close to renovated-home pricing for a home that still needs the work.

Notes & Caveats

The renovation gap

The two closest comps that beat this list price meaningfully were both renovated or updated. This home, as listed, has window and wall unit cooling and no mentioned kitchen or system updates. If you win this at Path B pricing, budget for eventual kitchen, bath, and cooling updates to bring it in line with what the top of this pocket's market actually looks like, that is also where the value-add upside lives if you plan to update it yourself.

Appraisal risk

Above roughly $1,900,000 to $1,950,000, expect real appraisal risk on an unrenovated home. Appraisers may lean on the broader, more original-condition sold set rather than the two renovated outliers. Plan for an appraisal gap and discuss the structure with your lender before submitting.

The nearby fixer listing

2846 Agua Vista Drive, same street, same bed/bath/sqft, is active at $1,700,000 but is a true fixer (no cooling, older roof), a step below this home's condition. Worth knowing about regardless of what you decide on 2838, since it affects your leverage and gives you a fallback if this one runs past your comfort level, but do not treat it as an identical alternative.
