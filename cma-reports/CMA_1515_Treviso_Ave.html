The Market Story

Here is the short version: homes like yours in this part of San Jose are selling fast and for strong numbers. Over the last six months, 32 single-family homes between 1,500 and 2,000 square feet have closed in 95118. The middle of that group sold for about $1,877,500, and the typical home went under contract in just seven days. When something is priced right around here, it does not sit.

What matters most for you is where your specific home fits. The cleanest read comes from the handful of newer Almaden Winery homes that have sold recently. The closest match is 5805 Chambertin Drive, which is the exact same size as yours at 1,756 square feet, built in the same era, and it closed at $2,010,000. A slightly larger neighbor at 1544 Bouchard Drive sold for $2,233,000. Both of these already price in the smaller lot and the HOA that come with this community, which is why I lean on them more heavily than the older ranch homes.

The older, larger-lot homes in the area sold roughly in the $1.7M to $2.1M range depending on condition and size. Your home has a few things working in its favor against that group: it was built in 1994 rather than the 1960s, it has 2.5 baths instead of the usual two, and from the last time it was on the market it showed updated kitchen and baths, hardwood floors, newer HVAC, and an EV-ready garage. The tradeoff is the smaller lot, which carries less land value than a 6,000 to 8,000 square foot parcel.

Put it all together and your home today lands in the low-to-mid two-million range. To give you a sense of the move: the last time this house was listed, back in 2019, the asking price was $1,399,000. Comparable homes are now closing around $2,000,000. That is meaningful equity growth, and it is the kind of number worth knowing whether you ever plan to sell, refinance, or just understand where you stand.

None of this is a push to do anything. It is a snapshot so you have real, current data instead of a guess. If you ever want to talk through what a sale would actually net you after costs, I am happy to run those numbers too.

Pricing Strategy Analysis

If you were to sell, here is how the three common approaches would likely play out in this market, based on what the recent sales actually did.

Price Below Market

List a touch under the comps to trigger a bidding war. In a market with a 7-day median and homes like Chambertin and Willowmont selling in 3 to 5 days, this strategy reliably draws multiple offers and often lands at or above true market value. It is the approach I would most likely recommend here, because the demand is clearly there.

Price At Market

List right at the value the comparable sales support, around the low-to-mid $2M range. This attracts serious buyers and still tends to sell quickly given how fast inventory is moving. Lower risk, slightly less upside than the bidding-war approach.

Price Above Market

List above the comps and hope a buyer pays a premium for the newer construction and updates. The risk is real: the few homes that sat longer, like 4450 Silva at 157 days, were the ones that started too high. Extra days on market usually cost you, they rarely help.

Things Worth Noting

Smaller lot, lower land value

Your lot is about 3,858 sq ft versus 6,000 to 8,000 for most area comps. Land carries value here, so I weighted the same-community sales (which share your lot profile) more heavily than the big-lot ranch homes.

Newer construction is a plus

Built in 1994, your home is decades newer than the typical 95118 comp. Newer systems, fewer deferred-maintenance surprises, and the EV-ready garage all appeal to today's buyers and help offset the lot difference.

2.5 baths and two stories

Most comps have two baths. Your extra half bath and efficient two-story layout are modest but genuine advantages when buyers compare options.

HOA

The $93/month Almaden Winery HOA is low and covers common-area maintenance. It is already reflected in the same-community comps, so it does not change the estimate.
