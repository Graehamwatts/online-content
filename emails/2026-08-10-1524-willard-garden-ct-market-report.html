Rates bottomed in December 2025 and have climbed back up since, driven in real part by the Iran conflict pushing oil prices and inflation higher. That is not a small factor: markets are now pricing meaningful odds (estimates range 65% to 82% depending on the source) of a Fed rate hike, not a cut, at the September meeting, a reversal from where expectations sat earlier this year. Housing economists are no longer expecting rates to fall below 6% in the near term. Higher rates hit condo buyers harder than single-family buyers in this price range, since first-time and move-up condo buyers are typically more payment-sensitive and less likely to be all-cash.

Looking further out: Fannie Mae, the MBA, and Wells Fargo all project rates gradually easing to roughly 6.2-6.4% through 2027. A more optimistic group of six major forecasters sees rates reaching 5.30-5.55% by mid-2027, but that outlook assumes the Iran conflict resolves and the Fed is able to resume cutting, neither of which is certain on that timeline.

The Honest 3-6 Month and 1-Year Outlook

The broader Bay Area single-family market is forecast to see modest appreciation, roughly 3-6% in 2026, possibly accelerating to 4-7% in 2027 if rates ease and conditions stay favorable. That forecast is for single-family homes, and condos have not been keeping pace with it. The data above shows Santa Clara County condo prices flat to down over the last year while single-family prices have continued to grind modestly upward, a real, current divergence between the two property types, not a temporary blip.

Putting that together honestly: even in the more optimistic scenario, where condos start participating in Bay Area appreciation again at something like the single-family rate, closing a gap of this size (roughly $95,000 to $146,000 depending on how you measure it) would take multiple years of sustained above-average growth, not the next two quarters. In the next 3 to 6 months specifically, the more likely near-term pressure is upward on rates, not downward, given where the Fed's September decision is trending. I don't see a data-backed case for waiting a few months and getting a meaningfully better outcome.
