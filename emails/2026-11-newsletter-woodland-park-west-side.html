Woodland Park's West Side Plan Goes to a Vote: What It Means for Your East Palo Alto Home

A 5-minute brief on the project the City Council put on hold, and the numbers that moved more than it did.

New on the channel
Small Scale. Big Reveal. 215 Newbridge St, Menlo Park

▷ Click the image to watch on YouTube

Hi,

On October 20 the East Palo Alto City Council put off its decision on Sand Hill Property Company's plan for the west side of Woodland Park. The vote was 4-0 to wait for a complete proposal. State housing law doesn't let the city wait long, so the council has to approve or reject it at its next meeting this month.

The plan is big. Sand Hill would renovate 315 apartments, tear down 144, and build 239 new apartments plus 60 townhomes for sale. The 7-Eleven and Three Brothers Tacos on that stretch would come down too.

Here's what I'd watch if you own in East Palo Alto. And what I wouldn't lose sleep over.

The headline number
60 townhomes
New homes for sale at market price on the west side of Woodland Park. Sand Hill's two Woodland Park pre-applications from this spring add up to 174 for-sale townhomes and 645 apartments.

3 things to know

1. The fight is over the affordable mix

The city's inclusionary ordinance would require 49 affordable rentals and 12 below-market townhomes. Sand Hill offered 124 affordable rentals and no below-market townhomes. The mayor said he couldn't accept losing the ownership units. That's the question the council has to settle this month, and the Planning Commission votes on the full project in the coming weeks.

2. A vote is not a shovel in the ground

One council member pointed to Sand Hill's stalled Euclid Improvements site, and speakers cited other projects that sat for years as construction costs climbed. A project this size takes years to permit, finance and build. If you're thinking about selling in the next year or two, these 60 townhomes are not your competition.

3. New townhomes become new comps

When they do sell, they'll be the newest for-sale homes on the west side. New construction usually sells for more per square foot than the older homes around it, and buyers and appraisers notice the comparison. For owners nearby I'd call that a modest plus, not a threat.

What this means for you specifically

Thinking about selling?
Homes here sold in an average of 31 days in September, at about full asking price. But the three-month median is down 10% from a year ago. Small samples swing, so I wouldn't panic. I would price off this fall's sales, not last year's.
Holding for the long run?
Nothing in this vote changes your plan. If your mortgage rate starts with a 3, 4 or 5, hang on to it. Reply and I'll show you where your equity stands today.
Buying in East Palo Alto?
Rates eased from 7.40% in mid-October to 7.24%. On an $800,000 loan that's about $87 a month. Homes are selling close to asking, so the rate is where your savings are hiding. Re-run your payment before you shop.
