If you own a home in East Palo Alto or anywhere on the mid-Peninsula, the Woodland Park development is the most important real estate story of the year for you. Not because it's making headlines — but because it isn't, yet.

772 new residential units are coming online over the next 18–24 months on a single EPA parcel. That's a meaningful supply event for a city of roughly 30,000 people. Here's what I've been telling my own clients about what that actually means for the value of the home you already own.

The headline number
772 units
≈ 2.5% increase in EPA's total housing supply over an 18–24 month delivery window

The 3 real effects on your home's value

1. Short-term price softening (next 12-18 months)

When supply rises faster than demand, prices flatten. Comparable Bay Area events (the 1,100-unit Balboa Reservoir delivery, the 5,700-unit Park Merced project) suggest EPA appreciation rates may temporarily slow to 0–2%/year versus the current 4–6% pace. Translation: if you're planning to sell in the next 12-18 months, the math changes.

2. Medium-term character change (24-36 months)

772 units doesn't just add housing. It adds infrastructure pressure, retail demand, traffic patterns, and school enrollment shifts. Properties closest to the development face mixed effects — some win on convenience (walkable amenities), some lose on quietness. Your block matters more than ever.

3. Long-term EPA upgrade (36+ months)

Larger residential projects historically raise the long-term value of surrounding single-family homes by signaling investor confidence and pulling commercial development. Atherton's adjacent value premium grew partly because of dense Menlo Park development, not despite it. The 5-year story is likely positive for EPA.
