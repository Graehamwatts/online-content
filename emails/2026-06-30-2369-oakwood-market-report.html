Pricing Analysis · Three Strategies

Based on those comps, here is my honest assessment of where 2369 Oakwood sits and the three ways we can price it. True market value lands at about $865,000.

Recommended
Aggressive · drive competition
$849,000

Price just under the field, create urgency, and let multiple buyers compete. In East Palo Alto's entry market an updated home with a garage under $850,000 draws a crowd, and competition often pushes the final number back up to or past $899,000.

Market · true value
$869,000

Priced right at value for a clean, normal-timeline sale. Most likely lands in the $860,000 to $880,000 range without much drama.

Aspirational · where we are now
$899,000

The top of what the home can defend. It can still sell here, but it asks for a patient buyer who values the updates and garage, and realistically negotiates into the mid $800s.

My Recommendation

The product here is a strong entry home, but the two-bedroom layout caps the buyer pool. Zero tours in eleven days at $899,000 is the market screening it out on price before anyone books a showing.

I would reprice to the aggressive band, around $849,000, to break that logjam. Counterintuitively, that is the move most likely to net you the highest final price, because it converts quiet disclosure interest into real tours and competing offers. If you would rather not lead with the most aggressive number, $869,000 at true market value is the conservative version of the same idea. Holding at $899,000 most likely buys a few more quiet weeks and then a reduction anyway, from a weaker position.

My honest read: move now, while we have two buyer camps already circling the disclosures.
