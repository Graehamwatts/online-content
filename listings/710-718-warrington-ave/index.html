Zoning

What the County zoning allows (M-1/NFO)

The County's zoning sorts what can happen on this land into three groups. Every item below should be verified with San Mateo County Planning and Building before you rely on it.

1. Allowed without a use permit

You can run these here without a use permit: indoor light manufacturing, storage of non-hazardous goods, R&D, artist studios, indoor wholesale and server farms. Construction and maintenance trades are allowed only inside an existing building.

Building permits still apply. A new building also needs design review (a Site Development Permit); moving one of these uses into an existing building needs no design review. Verify with the County.

2. Allowed only with a use permit

These need a use permit, which the County reviews case by case and can deny: live/work units; multi-family housing, but only on a parcel entirely within 200 ft of Fair Oaks Avenue; and brewing or distilling, restaurants and offices, but only as part of a mixed-use project (up to 25 percent of its floor area).

Verify with the County.

3. The existing house and duplex

The house on 710 and the duplex on 718 can keep being used as homes if they were legally built. The County calls this a legal nonconforming use. New single-family homes and duplexes are not allowed in this district.

Ask the County whether the current vacancy affects this status, and verify the permit history.

Ways to use the land

Four ways to use this land

From keeping what is there to building new. Paths 1 and 2 fit the zoning without a use permit; paths 3 and 4 need the County to approve a use permit first. Lot sketches show Warrington Ave on the left and Fair Oaks Ave at the top. Pictures are AI concept renderings, not designs.

1Keep and use what is there

Buy as is. Collect $5,500 a month from the rear shop, use the existing buildings for an allowed business, and fix up the house and duplex to live in or rent, if they were legally built. Ask the County whether outdoor yard use is allowed.

Best for: a maker, light industrial or small business that can work inside the buildings, or an investor holding the land.

Needs: no use permit for an allowed business; permits for repairs; a County check of the buildings' permit history.

2Build small-bay flex space

Clear the lots and build one single-story building of about 14,400 sq ft, split into six rentable bays with ten parking stalls, for trades, makers and small companies.

Best for: a developer or long-term investor who wants rent from several small tenants.

Needs: no use permit (light industrial is allowed); design review, building permits, utilities and frontage work.

3Build live/work units

About ten three-story units around a shared drive, each with a workshop or office on the ground floor and a home above. Ten units use about 21,000 sq ft, under the County's floor area limit of about 22,800.

Best for: a builder selling to owners who want to live where they work.

Needs: a use permit the County may or may not grant; each unit must be the occupant's main home and workplace.

4Build townhomes on 710

Townhome-style homes on 710. This counts as multi-family housing, which the County allows only on a parcel entirely within 200 ft of Fair Oaks Avenue, and 710 is close enough. Six homes are a placeholder; the County sets the number.

Best for: a residential builder.

Needs: a use permit, a County answer on the 50 ft lot width, and 1 to 2 covered parking spaces per home.

  5. Were the home, the duplex and the shop legally established, and are there permits on file?
