WHERE WE ARE TODAY
Starting with the good news, then the honest part.
Two offers in 90 days at $1,098,000 proves the price point is right. Both fall-outs were buyer financing — not the property.

Listed January 8 at $1,098,000. Into contract January 22 — fourteen days. Re-contracted March 13. Both fall-outs were on the buyer side: personal loan situations that fell apart in underwriting, not anything about the property itself. The home inspected, the value held, the price was acceptable to qualified buyers. The buyers' financial profiles just didn't make it through their own lender's process.

That distinction matters more than it looks on paper. In a balanced EPA market where homes typically close at 100–101% of asking, two buyer-financing fall-outs in 90 days means the property is doing exactly what it should — attracting qualified offers at the list price. The data point isn't "the price is wrong." The data point is "we need to vet buyers harder upfront and put the property in front of a stronger financial buyer pool."

That's the easiest problem in real estate to fix, and it's the central thing my marketing plan addresses.

Of 24 East Palo Alto comps in the last 6 months, zero were 3 bed / 1 bath that closed at $1,200,000 or higher.

Of 24 East Palo Alto single-family comps in the last 6 months, six were 3 bed / 1 bath. Their range was $771,000 to $1,125,000, with a median around $958,000.

The closest 3/1 to break the $1.2M ceiling was 2544 Illinois Street at $1,125,000 — and that one only got there with a full renovation, sold in 24 days.

The five comps that hit $1.2M+ all share the same recipe: 3 bed / 2 bath, permitted, fully updated. The bonus square footage in 2247 Menalto's rear unit is real value to a buyer who wants flexible living space — but it does not appraise, and the appraisal is what determines whether financing closes.

The data does not say "you can't sell at $1.2M." It says: at $1.2M, the buyer pool narrows to buyers who value the rear unit subjectively, plus cash buyers, plus strong-financial buyers who can absorb any appraisal gap with cash down. That pool exists — but given the two recent buyer-financing fall-outs, getting the right buyer in front of the property the first time is the entire game. That's what the marketing plan below is built to do.

HOW I'D MARKET 2247 MENALTO
A specific plan for reaching the smaller, slower, harder-to-reach buyer pool that pays the top of the range.
01

Premium Visual Package

Twilight photography, drone aerial showing the full lot and rear unit footprint, professional video walkthrough, dedicated single-property website. The current listing photos are competent but not premium — and at this price point, premium is the table stakes.

02

Geofenced Digital Campaign

Programmatic ads served to phones inside Belle Haven, Menlo Park, Stanford employee zones, and the Meta corporate campus. Plus retargeting for anyone who clicks. This puts the listing in front of the high-income buyer pool that values flex space.

03

6% Rate Buy-Down

Through preferred lender, qualifying buyers get a 6.0% rate vs the current 6.4–6.5% market rate, with closing costs covered. On a $1M loan, that saves a buyer roughly $250/month — which directly translates into payment-power that can lift the offer ceiling.

04

Direct Cash-Buyer + Investor Outreach

Curated list of EPA investor buyers and 1031 exchange clients. The rear unit is a positive for cash buyers (rental income, no financing sensitivity). I'll get the listing in front of 30–50 of them in week one — buyers who close on cash, fast, and without the personal-financing fragility we just saw twice.

05

Pre-Vetted Buyer Financing

Every offer that comes in goes through my preferred-lender pre-flight check before we accept. The two prior contracts collapsed in buyer underwriting — that's the single thing we have to prevent on round three. Pre-flight check means we know the buyer's loan is real before we take the property off the market.

06

Honest, Compliant Disclosure

Living area on MLS reflects permitted square footage. The rear unit is described in remarks and disclosures with full transparency. This protects the seller legally, protects the listing from future complaints, and positions the property correctly for the buyer pool that values it.
