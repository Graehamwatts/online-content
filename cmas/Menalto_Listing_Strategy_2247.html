HOW I'D MARKET 2247 MENALTO
A specific plan for reaching the small buyer pool that closed Westminster and Wisteria at $1.2M.
01

Premium Visual Package

Twilight photography, drone aerial, professional video walkthrough, dedicated single-property website. At $1.2M+ pricing, premium visuals are the table stakes.

02

Geofenced Digital Campaign

Programmatic ads served to phones inside Belle Haven, Menlo Park, Stanford employee zones, and the Meta corporate campus, plus retargeting. This reaches the high-income buyer pool that values flexible living space.

03

6% Rate Through Preferred Lender

Qualifying buyers get a 6.0% rate (not a buy-down — this is the actual lender-offered rate) vs the 6.4–6.5% prevailing market rate, with closing costs covered by the lender. On a $1M loan, that's roughly $250/month payment savings — translates directly to higher offer ceilings from the same buyer.

04

Direct Cash & Investor Outreach

Curated list of EPA investor buyers and 1031 exchange clients. The rear unit is a positive for cash buyers (rental income, no financing fragility). Listing in front of 30–50 qualified cash/investor buyers in week one.

05

Pre-Vetted Buyer Financing

Every offer goes through preferred-lender pre-flight check before acceptance. Prior contracts collapsed in buyer underwriting — that's the single thing we have to prevent on round three.

06

Honest, Compliant Disclosure

Permitted square footage in the Living Area field. Rear unit prominently described in remarks: "self-contained rear cottage with full kitchen, bath, and separate entry — buyer to verify permits and square footage." Captures value without compliance risk.

MY READ ON THE SITUATION

Putting all the data on one page: $1.2M is reachable for 2247 Menalto, but it is rare and structurally constrained right now. Two homes proved the price exists — Westminster (July 2024) and Wisteria — both 3 bed / 1 bath single families east of 101, both at almost identical size and lot to yours, both sold at exactly $1.2M. That's the recipe. The market has not changed materially since then; rates are actually 40 basis points lower today than when Westminster sold, and EPA median prices are roughly the same. So the calendar is not the obstacle.

The obstacle is 2383 Menalto Avenue. A 4 bed, 2 bath, fully remodeled home on a 7,252 sqft lot — same street, just up the road — listed at $1,198,000, reduced to $1,175,000, just went pending. The buyer pool the seller would be marketing to has already told us what they value bigger, better, fully-remodeled inventory on Menalto at: under $1,200,000. That's the most important number in this analysis. It is not a comp from twelve months ago — it is a real-time, same-street, in-progress signal of where buyers are willing to go.

Layered on top: 2247 Menalto's bonus rear unit is unpermitted, the main house is 3 bed / 1 bath in original condition, and any buyer is comparing against 2383 Menalto's renovated 4/2. The $1.2M target is not impossible, but it is a much narrower window than the $1.2M Westminster outcome, because Westminster did not have a same-street, larger, fully-remodeled active competitor pricing below the target.

The straight read: there is a path to a number with a 1.1 in front of it, and there is a much narrower path to a number with a 1.2 in front of it. Both require premium marketing, pre-vetted buyer financing, and the lender rate program. If we run all three correctly and we get the right buyer in the door before 2383 Menalto closes, we have a real shot at the higher number. If 2383 closes first and the appraiser uses it as a comparable, we are working uphill against a very recent, very local data point.

Here's how we run it together. I'll execute the aggressive marketing plan laid out above — premium photography and video, the geofenced digital campaign to the high-income buyer pool, direct outreach to investor and cash buyers, and the 6% lender rate program for any buyer who needs financing. We'll run open houses every weekend with a sign-in sheet and direct feedback collection. After every showing and every open house, I'll give you what buyers are actually saying — not a summary, the real comments. Every Monday you'll see the full dashboard of online views, disclosure downloads, showings, repeat showings, and offers. By the end of week two we will have a clear, data-backed read on where the market is willing to meet us. From there, we make decisions together based on what's actually happening, not on what either of us thought would happen.

That's the play. Happy to walk through any piece of it in more detail.
