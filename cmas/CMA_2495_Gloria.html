The Square-Footage Question. Why Two Numbers

County records list roughly 1,150 sq ft, which is almost certainly the permitted living area and the figure a lender's appraiser will use. The owner experiences the home as about 1,580 sq ft because the garage was converted, adding roughly 430 sq ft. The tell that the conversion is unpermitted: the garage door is still in place and just does not open. A permitted conversion almost always removes the door and finishes the opening as a wall. The carport out back covers the parking the garage used to provide, which is likely how the conversion happened.

IssueWhat it means for the sale
Appraisal gapA financed buyer's lender will likely appraise on the recorded ~1,150 sq ft. If a buyer agrees to a price that only pencils at 1,580 sq ft, the appraisal can come in low and the deal renegotiates, unless the buyer is cash or has a large down payment.
DisclosureWe disclose the conversion and its permit status openly. Handled this way it is a normal EPA situation; discovered late by an inspector it costs trust and deals.
The upside leverIf the conversion is permitted (or can be legalized), the 430 sq ft becomes fully creditable and we are firmly in the higher band. That is why confirming the permit is the single highest-value next step.

Both Ways, Side by Side, and the Permit Question

Here is the recommendation in one line: list at $1,035,000 to $1,100,000 as a 3 bed / 2 bath, ~1,580 sq ft, with the recorded 1,150 sq ft disclosed. What happens from there depends on the permit status, which is why that is the one thing to confirm:

If the conversion IS permitted
$1,100,000 to $1,175,000 (closer to $1.1M)
The 1,580 sq ft becomes fully creditable, so appraisers and financed buyers can support it. Even so, the comps and the 828 Weeks ceiling keep this realistic, not a $1.2M-plus number, given the minimal yard and softening market.
If it is NOT permitted (likely)
Realistically $975,000 to $1,035,000
Still list at 1,580 and disclose, but a financed buyer's appraisal holds the home near the recorded 1,150 sq ft. With the market softening (some comparable homes now selling below $1M), the realistic clearing sits around $975K to $1,035K. Only a cash or strong-down buyer who truly values the bonus space gets you above that.

Either way the downside is contained: even if it appraises to the recorded 1,150 sq ft, the floor value (roughly $975K to $1,035K) sits just below the list, so the risk of a deal blowing up is manageable, and the upside if a buyer values the bonus room is real. That is exactly why listing at 1,580 with full disclosure is the right play, see what the market gives us.
