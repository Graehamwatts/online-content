Where the Market Is. And What That Means for You

Closed sales in East Palo Alto stayed strong through this spring. The citywide average sale price firmed from roughly $1.03M in January 2026 to roughly $1.23M in April, homes have been clearing at or above asking when priced honestly, and the renovated 3 bed / 2 bath cottages near your size were the stars: 2512 Farrington (1,100 sq ft) cleared $1,325,000 in 10 days and 1371 Camellia (1,020 sq ft) cleared $1,240,000 in 19 days. The honest-but-not-flashy versions cleared in the $1.0M to $1.13M band, and ambitious pricing got punished with months of sitting.

But the picture as of this week is shifting, and you should price with eyes open. Active inventory has jumped (50 active, contingent, and pending residential listings citywide as of June 7, versus 29 single-family a week earlier), new listings are coming on faster than last spring, and several active small homes have just cut their prices: 219 Verbena from $999,000 to $950,000 after 82 days, 215 Wisteria from $975,000 to $945,000, 2246 Ralmar all the way from $898,000 to $820,000, and 2170 Ralmar relaunched $50,000 lower. A brand-new 3/1 at 1159 Oconnor just listed at $925,000, below where that cohort was closing a month ago. Sellers in the entry tier are blinking first. The full breakdown is in the market-direction section below, and it shapes the pricing recommendation: your window is good, but this is a price-it-right-and-go market, not a stretch-and-wait market.

Your home's value case is solid inside that picture. It is a true 3/2 (the stronger configuration), it presents in good condition, and the corner lot parks like a dream. The headwinds: the converted garage (likely unpermitted) makes appraisers cautious, the corner sits on a busier entry street, and the usable yard is modest. Your independent valuation of $1,145,800 lands between the value tier and the renovated stars, which matches what the walkthrough says about the home.

Notes & Caveats
The garage conversion (likely unpermitted) is the item to manage, not hide. The sealed-off garage adds real living utility but probably is not in the official 1,150 sq ft, and if it was done without permits it must be disclosed and most appraisers will give it limited value. The right play: market it as bonus space, disclose clearly, price the home on the recorded square footage with the bonus as upside, and have a rough permit-legalization cost ready for buyer conversations. Done this way it helps more than it hurts; discovered late by a buyer's inspector, it kills deals.

Corner lot on a busier entry street

Buyers will notice the traffic at the entry corner, and the usable yard is modest. The offsets are real: abundant parking (carport plus driveway), the detached laundry area, and corner-lot light. Pricing inside the recommended band already accounts for this; it is the main reason not to chase the Farrington number without the prep, especially with the market direction tightening.

Occupancy

Ownership is absentee, so confirm tenant status before listing. If tenant-occupied, plan notice, showing access, and ideally delivery vacant; East Palo Alto's tenant protections make this worth handling early and carefully, and every week of delay matters more in a softening active tier.
