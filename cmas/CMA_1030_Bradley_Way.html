The Real Question. Can You Break Even?
Honest answer: probably no, on a strict basis-to-net-sale basis. But on a cash-out basis (after counting 4 years of principal paydown), likely yes or close to it. And if primary-residence cap gains exclusion applies, any small gain is tax-free.

You bought 1030 Bradley Way on April 4, 2022 for $925,000. That was the top of the recent EPA cycle: the market peaked in mid-2022 at around $1.18M median for the broader SFR cohort, then cooled into 2023 as rates rose. Over the four years you have owned it, the EPA market for similar-sized homes has roughly traded sideways. So your accounting position vs the $925,000 basis is going to come down to where today's comps actually land.

The 800 to 1,100 sqft EPA cohort over the last six months shows a sold median of $977,500 and a sold range of $790K to $1,325K. But the cohort skews larger and 3-bed compared to your home. When you filter down to the most relevant comps (2-bed, 1-bath, under 1,050 sqft), the realistic clearing band drops to $850K to $925K. That is the band this CMA is built around.

What that means in cash terms:

  • At a likely sale of $895K and a standard 5.5% total friction (5% commission + 1.5% other costs cleaned of typo, total ~6.5%): net to you is roughly $837K, which is ~$88K below your $925K basis.
  • At the top of the realistic band, $925K sale at the same friction: net is roughly $865K, still ~$60K below basis.
  • With a sliding commission lever to 2.0% listing-side (total friction ~6%): the gap narrows by roughly $13K on a $900K sale. Helpful but does not change the bottom-line picture.

That sounds bad on its own. It is not the full picture. Principal paydown over 4 years on a 2022 mortgage is probably $30K to $55K in your favor (depending on the original loan size and rate; a buyer who locked early 2022 likely got a sub-4% rate). That cash sits in your equity, not on the closing statement, but it absolutely counts in your real net position. And if you have lived here two of the last five years, the IRS primary-residence capital gains exclusion ($250K single, $500K married) makes any positive gain tax-free.

The cleanest read: at a likely sale of $895K to $925K, your cash-out position is probably near break-even, plus or minus $20K, depending on your original loan structure and how long you actually lived here. That is a real number, not a marketing number, and it is the number to make the keep-or-sell decision against.

Three Paths. Honest Numbers on Each

Path A. List now, standard commission (~5.5% total friction)

Realistic clearing range: $850,000 to $925,000. Probable net to seller: $803K to $874K. Versus $925K basis, that is a $51K to $122K paper loss. Counting 4 years of principal paydown (~$30K-55K), real cash position is closer to break-even minus $20K to $90K, depending on the original loan structure. This is the "rip the band-aid off" path.

Path B. List now, sliding commission to 2.0% to 2.5% listing-side

Realistic clearing range: same $850,000 to $925,000. Pricing power does not change with commission structure. What changes is the friction: total friction drops from ~5.5% to ~6% down to ~4% to 4.5%. On a $900K sale that is roughly $13K to $14K of extra net to seller. Helpful, not transformative. Net to seller becomes roughly $812K to $886K. Real cash position improves to break-even minus $5K to $80K.

Commission concession should be a real negotiation lever for winning the listing and addressing his break-even concern directly. Use it openly, not as sleight of hand.

Path C. Rent and revisit in 2 to 3 years (strongly worth considering)

EPA market rents for an 810 sqft 2/1 cottage in this area: roughly $2,400 to $2,800 per month based on small-SFR rental comps (verify with a rental scrape if pursuing). At his original 2022 mortgage rate (likely sub-4%) the principal+interest payment is probably $3,000 to $3,800 per month. Rental income may cover most or all of debt service depending on his original loan amount, with the rest absorbed by tax-deductible interest and the principal-paydown forced savings. Two to three years of patience: market normalizes, rates likely soften, and the same $925K basis becomes a comfortable $20K to $80K gain (assuming 2% to 3% annual EPA appreciation, conservative). This is the path that protects his $925K basis without forcing a sale at a soft moment.

Notes & Caveats

About the AVM versus the comps

The CoreLogic AVM mid of $993,000 came up materially higher than what the actual sold comps support ($850K-$925K realistic range). The AVM confidence score was 54/100, low. AVMs lean on broader cohort averages and don't always weight the relevant comp set correctly. The comps are the ground truth here. If we go to market based on the AVM, we overprice and chase the market down. The CMA range is the right anchor.

Capital gains exclusion. Worth verifying.

If Hu lived at 1030 Bradley as his primary residence for at least 2 of the last 5 years, IRS Section 121 lets him exclude up to $250K of gain (single) or $500K (married filing jointly). His tax record currently shows owner-occupied, suggesting he qualifies. If he qualifies, any cash-out gain is tax-free. He should confirm with his CPA before deciding.

Loan structure matters for the rent-and-wait math

The exact monthly carrying cost depends on his original loan amount, rate, and term, which we don't have. Before committing to Path C (rent and revisit), pull his loan statement and run a real cash-flow scenario at expected rent of $2,400 to $2,800 per month.
