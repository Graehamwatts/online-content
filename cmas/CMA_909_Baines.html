909 Baines is a two-story, four-bedroom detached home in the Ravenswood Villages development, built in 2000. That vintage matters: it is one of the newer homes in East Palo Alto, where most of the stock dates to the 1950s, and it shares its era and style with the strongest recent sales in the area. At 2,020 square feet with a two-and-a-half-bath layout, high ceilings, and a kitchen island, it offers more finished space than the older cottages that make up most of the local inventory. This review is meant as a grounded, pre-market read on value, deliberately kept on the conservative side, so you have a clear sense of where the home stands before any decision about selling.

Where the Market Is. And What That Means for You

East Palo Alto's single-family market has firmed up over the last year. The average sale price across the city's SFR cohort climbed steadily through the first four months of 2026, from roughly $1.03M in January to roughly $1.23M in April, and homes are clearing at or above asking nearly every month. Spring is the strongest demand window of the year on the Peninsula, so the backdrop for a home like yours is favorable.

The number that matters most is where homes like yours have actually cleared. The newer, 2,000-square-foot-class four-bedrooms in East Palo Alto, the same vintage as your Ravenswood Villages home, have been selling between roughly $1.31M and $1.48M. The independent automated valuation puts your home at $1,469,700, near the top of that band. For a pre-market read, I am centering the suggested value a step below that, around $1.40M to $1.45M, to keep it realistic and conservative rather than aspirational.

The competition picture is in your favor. There is very little fresh, well-priced inventory in your lane right now. The two active homes priced near this level, 950 Mouton Circle at $1,549,000 and 1120 Alberni at $1,425,000, have been sitting for 62 and 117 days, which is what an ambitious price buys you here. Below you sits a wall of smaller three-bedroom, one-bath homes from $898K to $1.15M. Your home is a clear step up from that group.

Mortgage rates sit around 6.5% today, down from the 7%-plus peaks of 2023 but still well above the 2021 lows. That has thinned the buyer pool at the very top of the market, but it has not stopped well-presented, fairly priced East Palo Alto homes from selling fast and over asking. The takeaway: your home sits comfortably in the upper tier of the EPA market, and a conservative, well-supported number is the right way to frame it before going public.

Three Paths Forward. The Real Trade-Off

Because this is an investment property rather than your residence, the decision is really about what you want this capital to do next. Three honest paths:

Path A. Sell now, take the gain, redeploy (if your goal is to exit)

Realistic clearing range: $1,399,000 to $1,475,000. The spring market is strong, direct competition in your lane is thin, and the home is a newer, larger four-bedroom that stands out from the older stock. List in the competitive band, run a standard two-to-three week marketing window, and let the demand drive a fast, at-or-over-asking sale. Vacating the home before listing (see the occupancy note below) will widen the buyer pool and help you reach the top of this range.

Path B. Optimize for the top of the range. Light prep, then list.

Realistic clearing range: $1,475,000 to $1,575,000. The gap between your home and the $1.475M-plus Wilks and Oakes sales is finish. If the interior is dated from tenant use, a targeted refresh (fresh neutral paint, professional clean, updating any worn flooring or fixtures, and light staging) can move you toward those numbers. With the home vacant and the spring window open, this prep can happen inside a few weeks. The trade is a modest budget and a short delay for a real shot at the upper band.

Path C. Keep it as a rental. For a long-term holder.

You already hold this as an investment, so continuing is a real option. A newer 2,020 sq ft four-bedroom in this area rents in the range of roughly $4,500 to $5,500 a month. Against a low Proposition 13 tax basis, the carry is favorable, and East Palo Alto rents have held up. The case for selling instead is that the equity here is large relative to the rent yield, and that capital may work harder redeployed. The case for holding is continued appreciation, depreciation benefits, and not triggering the tax on the gain. This is a portfolio decision, and the numbers below help frame it.

Notes & Caveats
Occupancy and showing access. Tax records show an absentee owner, so the home is most likely tenant-occupied or held as an investment. Before any listing, confirm whether a tenant is in place and the terms of any lease. A tenant-occupied home limits showing access and can narrow the buyer pool, and California rules govern notice and relocation if a tenant must move. The cleanest path to the top of the range is to deliver the home vacant and refreshed. If a lease runs past a likely close date, that needs to be factored into both timing and price.

Condition is the swing factor

The value above assumes a condition consistent with the comp set. The 2000 build, high ceilings, and original kitchen island are strengths, but if the interior is worn from rental use, that pulls toward the conservative band. A modest refresh, covered in Path B, is what unlocks the Wilks and Oakes level. Confirming the current condition is the single input that tightens this range.

Capital gains. This is an investment property.

Your Proposition 13 basis suggests a purchase around $570,000 to $620,000, so a sale near $1.4M to $1.45M implies a large taxable gain. Because the home is not your primary residence, the IRS Section 121 exclusion does not apply. The gain would be taxable, and depreciation taken over the years is subject to recapture. A 1031 exchange into another investment property is the main tool to defer that tax if you plan to redeploy. Talk to your CPA before deciding, because the tax treatment can materially change which path makes sense.
