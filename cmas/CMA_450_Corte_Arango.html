Pricing Strategy: How I Would Position It

A multifamily building is priced differently from a home. Investors run the numbers before they ever walk it, so the list price has to correspond to a cap rate they will accept, or the building sits and goes stale. Here is how I would approach it.

Price To Sell / Compete
List ~$1,195,000
A hair under the midpoint. Pencils at roughly a 6% cap, which is genuinely attractive here and would pull multiple investor offers, potentially bidding the price up toward the mid $1.2s.
Price At Market (my likely recommendation)
List ~$1,295,000
Leaves negotiating room while staying in acceptable-cap territory. Supported by the rent-upside story on the un-updated unit. Expect it to trade around $1.2M to $1.25M.
Price Aspirational
List $1,400,000+
Reaches for a top number, but it prices the deal at a sub-5% cap on current rents. Investor buyers will pass, and the listing risks sitting and needing a cut, the classic multifamily overpricing trap.
The lever that moves the top end: if the rents can be pushed, the whole picture improves. The updated units already support ~$1,900. If market rent is really closer to $2,000 to $2,100 as leases turn, a buyer underwriting to that pro-forma income could justify the $1.35M-plus range. That is the value-add story we would market, and it is another reason your actual rent roll matters, it tells us exactly how much upside is real versus already captured.

Because the buyer is an investor, three things drive your outcome: the rents (higher in-place income equals higher price), the condition (your updated units mean a buyer inherits fewer immediate costs, which supports a lower cap rate and a higher price), and the pricing (list it at a cap rate that pencils and it moves; overprice it and investors simply skip it). Interest rates matter here too, commercial multifamily loans price off different indexes than home loans, and at today's rates buyers are disciplined on cap rate, which is exactly why starting at a realistic number is what actually captures the best price.
