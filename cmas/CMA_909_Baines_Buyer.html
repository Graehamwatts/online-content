How 909 Baines Stacks Up. And the One Comp That Matters Most

At 2,020 sq ft, a 2000 build, and good condition, this home sits in the upper part of its comp range. The smaller, remodeled 2000-vintage twins (928 Wilks and 939 Oakes) cleared $1.475M to $1.48M at $783 to $809 per square foot, but they are turnkey and smaller. The similar-size four-bedrooms (2370 Ralmar and 2212 Dumbarton at about 1,960 sq ft) cleared $1.31M to $1.38M at $667 to $703 per square foot. A 2,020 sq ft home of this vintage that shows well reasonably supports roughly $1.43M to $1.47M, which is above the $1,399,888 asking price. That gap, a list price set below supportable value on a good-condition home, is exactly why this should be treated as a competitive situation rather than a negotiation to chip the price down.

The most important comp is 639 Montage. It listed at $1,399,000, the same number as 909 Baines, and sold $41,000 OVER asking at $1,440,000. That is the precedent that says if this home draws interest, you should plan to compete at or slightly above asking, not below it. Pricing a home at a credible number to invite competition is a common Peninsula tactic, and 2370 Ralmar (listed at $1,198,000, sold $112K over in 8 days) is the more aggressive version of the same playbook.
