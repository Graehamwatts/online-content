The Rent Path, Honestly Run

Your agent's suggestion to rent is not crazy, so here is the math done fairly. A 1,580 sq ft 2 bed, 2.5 bath townhome in a gated East Palo Alto community minutes from Meta and Stanford would likely rent for roughly $3,800 to $4,500 a month (estimate, verify against current rental listings before deciding).

If there is a mortgage on the unit, the payment comes out of that number and the monthly cash flow likely lands near zero or negative. And three bigger things sit underneath the monthly math:

1. The tax clock. Move out and rent it, and your Section 121 exclusion generally survives only if you sell within three years of moving out. Rent longer and your roughly $80,000 to $105,000 of tax-free gain becomes taxable, plus depreciation recapture on top. The rent path quietly costs you the tax shelter you have already earned.

2. You become a landlord in a rent-controlled, tenant-protective city. East Palo Alto has some of the strongest tenant protections in the state. Getting a tenant out later, if you change your mind and want to sell, is slow and can require relocation payments. The rent decision is much harder to reverse than it sounds.

3. The yield is thin. Roughly $25,000 to $27,000 a year of pre-mortgage net cash flow against roughly $900,000 of value is about a 3% yield, before tenant turnover, special assessments, or repairs. Your equity can likely earn that with far less work elsewhere.

The Honest Bottom Line

The data says your home can sell. The current listing just is not selling it.

Your identical floor plan went into contract in 20 days at a higher price while yours sat. That is not a dead market, that is a fixable listing. The playbook: refresh the presentation (professional photos, staging, paint touch-ups), relaunch with real marketing reach, and price in the $899,000 to $925,000 band where your own complex just proved demand exists. Done right, the most likely outcome is a sale in the $905,000 to $930,000 range with your gain fully tax-sheltered.

If you do prefer to rent, do it with open eyes

It can work for a committed long-term holder. But run it knowing the cash flow is thin after costs, the Section 121 shelter erodes after three years as a rental, and East Palo Alto tenant protections make the decision hard to unwind. Renting because the current listing stalled is solving the wrong problem.
