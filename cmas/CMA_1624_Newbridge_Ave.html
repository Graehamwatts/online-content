The Market Story

San Mateo single family homes are having one of their strongest runs in years. Across the city, homes sold for 110 to 113 percent of their asking price this spring, the highest sale-to-list ratios in the last three and a half years, and inventory sat at just 0.6 months of supply in June, the tightest reading in the entire three year data set. Under three months of supply is considered a seller's market. At 0.6, buyers are competing hard for every well-priced home.

Your pocket, South Shoreview, shows this vividly. Two homes with essentially your exact configuration, 3 bedrooms, 1 bath, 1,000 square feet on a 5,000 square foot lot, have sold nearby in the last five months: 1952 Shoreview Avenue closed at $1,399,000 in February after 7 days on market, and 1825 Dale Avenue closed at $1,355,000 on July 8, just days ago, after 5 days on market. Every recent Shoreview-pocket sale went over its asking price, most by double digits.

Your own street shows the ceiling too. 1757 Newbridge Avenue, renovated top to bottom with new windows, plumbing, and electrical, closed at $1,639,900 in April. That is what a fully done version of a home like yours trades for in this market.
