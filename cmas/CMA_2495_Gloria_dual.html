The Square-Footage Question. Why Two Numbers

The single biggest variable in this home's value is whether that converted garage counts as living area, and right now the honest answer is: partly, and at a discount. Here is the reasoning.

County records list roughly 1,150 sq ft. That is the figure a lender's appraiser will rely on, and it is almost certainly the permitted, legal living area. The owner experiences the home as about 1,580 sq ft because the garage was converted into living space, adding roughly 430 sq ft. The tell that the conversion is unpermitted: the garage door is still in place and simply does not open. A permitted conversion almost always removes the garage door and finishes the opening as a wall, because the city requires it. The carport in the back covers the parking the garage used to provide, and East Palo Alto historically allowed carports to satisfy parking, which is likely how the conversion happened in the first place.

What this means in plain terms:

IssueWhat it means for the sale
Appraisal gapA financed buyer's lender will likely appraise on the recorded ~1,150 sq ft. If a buyer agrees to pay a price that only makes sense at 1,580 sq ft, the appraisal can come in low and the deal renegotiates or falls apart, unless the buyer is paying cash or has a large down payment.
DisclosureThe conversion and its unknown permit status must be disclosed. Handled openly it is a normal EPA situation; discovered late by a buyer's inspector it kills trust and deals.
The upside leverIf the space can be permitted / legalized (or even just documented with a clear contractor path), the 430 sq ft becomes far easier for buyers and appraisers to credit, which is what unlocks Scenario B value.

Action before listing

Pull the permit history from the City of East Palo Alto for 2495 Gloria Way and confirm exactly what is on record. That single step tells us which scenario is real, and it is the highest-leverage thing we can do to firm up the price. Confirm the measured square footage on the walk-through as well.

Putting the Two Together. Recommended Strategy

The honest value of this home is a band, not a point, and the band runs from roughly $1,049,000 (recorded, conservative) to roughly $1,275,000 (full bonus credit, best case). The spread of about $150,000 to $200,000 is almost entirely the converted-garage permit question. Here is how to capture the most of it without a deal blowing up on appraisal:

1. Pull the permits first (biggest lever)

Confirm the conversion's status with the City of East Palo Alto before listing. If it is permitted, we market 1,580 sq ft with confidence and price into Scenario B. If it is not, we get a contractor estimate to legalize it so buyers can price the path, which alone moves buyers toward the higher band.

2. List in the bridge, market the space honestly

Absent a permit resolution before listing, price in the bridge between the two scenarios, around $1,099,000 to $1,150,000, and market the home as a 3 bed / 2 bath plus a large converted bonus room with permit status disclosed and to be verified by buyer. That headline attracts the buyers who value the extra space while keeping a defensible recorded-value floor for financed offers.

3. Let the buyer pool sort it

Expect cash and strong-down buyers to pay toward Scenario B for the usable space, and financed buyers to be held nearer Scenario A by their appraisal. Pricing in the bridge invites both to the table and lets competition push the final number up.

Notes & Caveats

Pull the EPA permit record, confirm the measurement, disclose clearly, and decide between legalizing or pricing-and-disclosing before going live. This single item drives which scenario is real.

Tenant and possession

About a 16-year tenancy, with move-out scheduled around now. A long EPA tenancy carries rent-control / just-cause / relocation considerations, so confirm the move-out is clean and documented and that the home delivers vacant before listing.
