Prior Listing. What the Market Told Us
Listed 02/04/2026 at $1,248,888 · Withdrawn 03/26/2026 after 50 days on market · no acceptable offers received.

The prior list price of $1,248,888 ($638/sqft) was already aggressively below where the same size, same area sells without complications. For context: EPA single-family homes 1,500 to 2,000 sq ft sold $771K to $1,325K over the last 6 months at a median of $1,046K and $887/sqft. The prior list was already at the floor of that range despite this property being substantially larger on land and having a brand-new back unit.

So price was not the failure. The blockers were structural:

The tenant in the front unit

For a retail owner-occupant buyer (the largest pool in EPA), an occupied unit creates real friction: showings, lease assumption or relocation risk, eviction concerns, financing/appraisal complications. Buyer feedback during the listing centered exactly on this.

Two homes on one lot. A feature for the right buyer.

The dual-home configuration is genuinely something a specific buyer will love: an investor wanting a fully-permitted income property, a multi-generational family who needs separate quarters for parents or adult children, or an owner-occupant who lives in one and rents the other. It is not a flaw, it just narrows the audience. The job is reaching that buyer, not lowering price for a buyer who would never want this configuration anyway.

The rear home is a real asset. Do not underestimate it.

You invested approximately $400,000 to $500,000 in the brand-new back home: modern open-concept design, soaring wood ceilings, spa-style baths, motorized awning patio, solar on both units, indoor/outdoor flow, and a garage. It shows in top condition. The front 1919 home is older and tenant-occupied (clean and livable, with some renovation upside), but the back home alone represents a substantial portion of total value. Pricing must reflect that recent investment.

The market did not reject the price. It rejected the marketing approach for this kind of property. The right play is targeting the audience this home actually fits: investors, multi-gen families, or creative owner-occupants. That distinction matters for the path forward.

The Market Story

East Palo Alto's broader SFR market is active but rate-constrained. Over the last six months, the 1,500–2,000 sq ft cohort closed $771K to $1,325K with a median sold around $1,046K and median DOM 23. Active 3-bed/2-bath listings in the area sit in the $1.05M to $1.30M range; 3-bed/1-bath in the $900K to $1M range. The over-asking premium that defined 2021–22 has cooled to roughly at-asking now, a rate-driven shift across the Peninsula.

Within that EPA market, this property is genuinely unique: the 12,045 sq ft lot is materially larger than any recent comparable sale, and the brand-new back home is a real value driver. Pure 2-on-1 dual-home comps in EPA are too sparse to triangulate cleanly, so this analysis leans on the broader EPA SFR cohort, the property's own prior-listing market test, and judgment-based adjustments for the unique features.

This property has real value, but the right marketing angle matters more than another price cut. Repositioned as an investment / multi-family-feel property with cap-rate framing (Option C below), value lands around $1.30M–$1.45M with the tenant in place, supported by income-approach math at typical EPA cap rates and by the rear home's recent build cost. With the tenant out and the back home featured as a clean SFR-plus-ADU package, value moves to $1.40M–$1.60M, which is where your $1.6M target most cleanly lands.

What this means for 2260 Clarke

Elevated rates have thinned the typical RETAIL buyer pool across EPA. Investor buyers (who often pay cash or underwrite at cap rates rather than monthly affordability) are much less rate-sensitive than retail. That is the central insight here: pivoting to investor-targeted marketing (Option C below) sidesteps the rate problem entirely. The slight downward drift in rates also helps either path going into the next listing.

Strategic Options. Three Paths Forward

Option A. Re-list AS-IS, tenant in place

Realistic clearing price: around $1.4M, give or take. With a tenant in place and no other changes, the realistic selling band for a property like this lands in the $1,350,000 to $1,450,000 zone. The rear home alone represents roughly $400K to $500K of recent build, plus the front home and 12,045 sq ft of land, which is why this band sits above the broader EPA SFR median. Buyer pool is narrower than a clean retail listing, so expect DOM in the 30 to 60 day range.

Option B. Vacate tenant first, then re-list (max top-line)

Achievable range: $1,400,000 to $1,600,000. Removing the tenant transforms the package into a clean SFR-plus-ADU (or two-homes-vacant) presentation: full retail buyer pool, lender-friendly. Cost side: relocation incentive (cash-for-keys typically $10K to $25K) plus possibly 60 to 90 days of vacancy before listing. The back home is already staged, so that cost is already absorbed. Math: a $200K to $400K price lift versus relocation cost is a high-ROI trade. This is the path that puts your $1.6M target most cleanly in range.

Option C. List as investment / multi-family-feel property, cap-rate priced (recommended)

Achievable range: $1,300,000 to $1,600,000+ depending on rents. Even though technically zoned SFR, the property markets cleanly as a 2-on-1 income asset with cap-rate framing. Notes & Caveats

How this analysis works without direct comps

True 2-on-1 dual-home EPA comps don't exist in the recent dataset, so this analysis triangulates from three angles per CMA best practice: (1) adjacent-cohort baseline (EPA SFR 1,500 to 2,000 sqft) with qualitative adjustments for the unique features; (2) the rear home's recent build cost (~$400K to $500K invested) as a concrete value floor; (3) income approach at standard EPA cap rates against expected rents. The prior listing's 50-day market test is itself the strongest property-specific data point. The result is wider-banded than a typical CMA, which is honest, not a hedge.
