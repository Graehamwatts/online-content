Renovating lifts the price by roughly $250,000 but costs about $100,000 plus carrying time and execution risk, so the net gain is on the order of $150,000 before selling costs. For a long-held or estate seller who wants a clean, fast exit, listing as-is at a sharp number near $1.2M is usually the better path: it draws the contractor and investor pool immediately and avoids tying up cash in a remodel. The renovate-first path only makes sense if the seller has the time, capital, and appetite for the project.

Why $1.8M to $2M Does Not Pencil

The price expectation is worth walking through, because it shapes how we position the home. A $1.8M to $2M target is well above anything the East Palo Alto sales support for a home like this, and the MLS makes the reason concrete.

  • Of all 19 comparable sales in the past year, exactly one closed above $1,550,000: 206 Donohoe Street at $1,925,000. It sold high because it sits on a 9,133 square foot lot, nearly double this one, and was marketed as a two-unit property. Lot size and a second unit, not finishes, produced that number.
  • The next highest, 2238 Lincoln at $1,531,000, also sat on an oversized 10,585 square foot lot.
  • Every fully renovated or newer-built 4-bedroom home topped out between roughly $1,310,000 and $1,480,000.
  • No original-condition home on a standard lot, which is exactly what 1419 Kavanaugh is, sold above $1,230,000 all year.

So in this market the path past the high $1M range runs through lot size, extra units, or new construction, none of which a $100,000 cosmetic renovation provides on a 5,500 square foot lot. Listing 1419 Kavanaugh at $1.8M would draw few showings, accumulate days on market, and most likely sell for less than a sharply priced launch near $1.2M would have. The fastest route to the strongest number is to price it where the comps defend it and let the size advantage and the fast spring market create competition.

Diligence Before Listing
Two items to confirm, both of which can move the number:
  • Permitted square footage. The 2,050 sq ft figure is the single biggest value lever. If part of it is unpermitted, the defensible value drops toward the smaller-home comps. Pull the permit history and confirm before we set the list price.
  • Vesting and authority to sell. The assessor record shows the Versie L. Williams Trust with a very low Prop 13 basis, which points to a long-held or estate situation. Confirm the trustee or executor has authority and whether a probate process applies, since that affects timeline and terms.
Recommendation

List as-is around $1,195,000 to $1,250,000 to draw the contractor, investor, and renovate-it-yourself owner-occupant pool from day one. In this fast spring market a sharp launch in that band should produce competition and a sale near or a touch above $1.2M, quickly and cleanly. If the seller is set on a higher number, the honest answer from the closed sales is that the value is not there as-is, and the only way to chase it is a full renovation first, which nets only modestly more.
