The Bottom Line, Up Front

430 Garden Street is a 1,030 square foot home on an unusually large 10,113 square foot lot, nearly double the typical East Palo Alto parcel. In today's market the right list price is in the high $900s to about $1,000,000, and the oversized lot is the feature that pushes it to the top of that range.

  • What homes like this are selling for: over the last six months, 16 East Palo Alto homes in this size class sold from $760,000 to $1,325,000, a median of $957,500 and a median of about $899 per square foot.

  • My recommended list: price it at $998,000 to invite competition, with a realistic sale around $1,000,000 to $1,050,000 in a market carrying only two months of inventory.
Why list in the high $900s, not higher or lower. East Palo Alto is a seller's market right now (about two months of inventory, homes closing near 97 percent of list). Acre Lot

A classic single-story East Palo Alto home on a deep, oversized lot. The structure is modest at roughly 1,030 square feet, so the value here is driven by location and land rather than the house itself. The lot is the differentiator, most homes in this size class sit on 5,000 square foot parcels, and this one is more than twice that, with room for an accessory dwelling unit (ADU) and a large yard. Inside, a rear sunroom has been converted into a flexible bonus room that functions as a third bedroom, a real livability plus even though it is not permitted as a legal bedroom.

The Market Story

East Palo Alto is moving fast right now. There is only about two months of inventory on the ground, and homes are closing at roughly 97 percent of their list price. When a home in this size class is priced right, it does not sit. Six of the sixteen recent sales closed in nine days or fewer, several of them over a million dollars.

For a home like 430 Garden, the comparable pool is tight and tells a clear story. A near-twin at 2509 Illinois, also 1,030 square feet and a 3 bed / 1 bath, sold for $955,000 in February on a 6,000 square foot lot. Two smaller homes did better: 2055 Oakwood at 990 square feet brought $1,035,000, and 2670 Illinois at 970 square feet brought $1,000,000. Garden Street has more living-adjacent land than any of them, which is why I see it competing at the top of that cluster rather than the middle.

The fast money went to clean, well-priced homes. The two that sat, 2060 Pulgas at 55 days and 128 Grace at a painful 138 days, were larger or more dated homes that started too high and had to wait for the market to catch up. That is the single most important lesson for pricing Garden Street: in this market, a sharp price creates urgency and competition, while an ambitious price creates silence.

The active and pending listings confirm it. Homes in contract right now (pending) cluster around $968,000 to $1,050,000, while the active homes that have sat 75 to 98 days are the ones that reached for too much early. There is genuine buyer demand in the high $900s and right around a million for this product, and the oversized lot gives Garden Street a story none of those competitors can match.

Pricing Strategy

The data points to three ways to price Garden Street. I recommend the middle path, a sharp list that draws competition, because that is what this market is rewarding.

Strategy 1, price to compete
$975,000 to $998,000
A sharp number that lands below the round million and pulls in multiple buyers. In a two-month-inventory market this is what creates a bidding situation and pushes the final price up. The fast sales in the comp set all used this approach. My recommendation.
Strategy 2, price at market
$999,000 to $1,025,000
Priced right at where the closest comps and current pendings sit. Fair and defensible, but it leaves less room for buyers to compete upward and can sell at, rather than above, list.
Strategy 3, price ambitious
$1,075,000+
Reaches for the top of the range on the strength of the lot. The risk is real: two comps that started high sat 55 and 138 days. Only justified if condition is strong and we are willing to wait.

Special Considerations

The oversized lot and ADU potential

At 10,113 square feet, this lot is roughly double the East Palo Alto norm. That supports an accessory dwelling unit, which adds both value and future rental income, and it is the single strongest selling point. Marketing should lead with the land, not the 1,030 square foot house.

The bonus room (a converted sunroom)

The home lives as a three-bedroom thanks to a rear sunroom that was converted into a usable bonus room, but it is not permitted as a bedroom, so the county and an appraiser will count the home as a legal two-bedroom. In practice this rarely costs much: buyers feel the extra usable space and the flexibility (office, nursery, guest room). The right play is to market it honestly as a bonus room or flexible third room, disclose the unpermitted status up front, and let the livability sell itself.

Condition and the 1923 vintage

The home dates to 1923 and has been investor-held and tenant-occupied. Condition will swing the final number more than any comp: a clean, updated presentation supports the stretch range, while deferred maintenance pulls it toward the conservative range. A pre-list walkthrough will let us place it precisely.

Tenant occupancy

If the home is currently rented, showing access and the buyer pool both narrow (more investors, fewer owner-occupants). Delivering it vacant, or with flexible showing, typically widens demand and lifts the price. Worth planning before we go live.

Rate and market timing

Inventory is tight at about two months and homes are closing near 97 percent of list, a genuine seller's market. Mortgage rates remain the main demand lever, but at this price point East Palo Alto continues to draw strong first-time-buyer and investor interest. Listing into low inventory favors the seller.
