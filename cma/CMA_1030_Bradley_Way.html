Price Reductions: What The Data Actually Shows

Only one closed comp in this set needed a price cut to sell: 1133 Jervis Ave. It is worth looking at closely, because it is the clearest real-world example of what a reduction looks like and what it accomplishes here.

1133 Jervis Ave, the one real price-reduction case study: listed 9/6/2025 at an original price of $888,000. After sitting past the point where similar homes were going pending, the list price was cut to $866,666, a reduction of $21,334 (-2.4%). It sold at $860,000, which is 99.2% of the reduced list and 96.8% of the original list. Total time from list to accepted offer was well past this segment's 27-day average. The lesson: it did not take a dramatic cut to get this home moving again, a modest, realistic adjustment in the 2 to 3 percent range was enough.

Every other sold comp in this data set closed without a price reduction, most of them quickly (6 to 25 days), but nearly all of those closings happened before the market softened in H2 2025. Jervis is the closest precedent to today's conditions, and the Jervis math (roughly 2 to 3 percent below original list) lands almost exactly on the $900,000 already offered on this house. That is worth sitting with: the market's own precedent for "what it takes to sell in a softer window" and the actual offer you already have are telling you the same number.
