The Condo Conversion Question, Answered Honestly

This is the part I know you're most curious about, so I want to walk through it plainly rather than just say "it's possible."

The Good News
Your building is zoned RH2 and is a genuine legal 2-unit, which means it qualifies in principle for San Francisco's "2-Unit Bypass," the fastest path to condo conversion in the city. Unlike buildings with 3 to 6 units, a duplex does not have to wait for the annual lottery, which has been on hiatus since 2020 and, as of today, still hasn't resumed. If the lottery is your only other option, the bypass is genuinely the better path available right now.
The Real Catch
The 2-Unit Bypass was built for TIC arrangements: two separate owners, each holding at least a 25% interest, each occupying their own unit continuously for at least a year. You own the whole building together, through one trust, as a married couple, not as two separate co-owners in separate units. Whether your specific ownership structure still qualifies, or whether it would need to be restructured first, is a real legal question, not something I can answer with certainty from here.
My honest recommendation: talk to a condo conversion attorney (Andy Sirkin's office in San Francisco specializes specifically in this) before assuming either way. If you do qualify, or can restructure ownership to qualify, the upside case in the comparison below is real and worth planning around. If you don't qualify under the current bypass rules, the citywide lottery-based Expedited Conversion Program is the fallback, but it has been suspended since 2020 with no confirmed return date as of this report, so that path is a genuine "wait and see," not a near-term option.
One more disclosure item worth remembering: the 2012 listing for this exact address noted the lower unit was tenant-occupied under protected rent-control status at the time. If your building has any occupancy or eviction history since, that's specifically relevant to bypass eligibility, since the program has a clean-history requirement around elderly, disabled, or catastrophically ill tenants. Worth confirming with your attorney alongside the ownership-structure question.

Worth noting: two of the comps below, 1106 Masonic Ave and 1346 Masonic Ave, both blocks from you, were marketed with MLS remarks explicitly flagging condo-conversion potential. That doesn't resolve your ownership-structure question, but it confirms agents and buyers in this exact neighborhood already treat comparable duplexes as live conversion candidates.
