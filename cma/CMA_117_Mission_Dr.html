San Francisco is a different animal, and worth naming so nobody draws the wrong conclusion from headlines. SF single-family homes are up over 22% year over year to a $2.2M median, selling in about 12 days at nearly 25% over asking, and even SF condos have turned the corner, now selling in about 16 days at roughly 7% over asking. That recovery is being driven by the AI hiring wave, return-to-office mandates, and sky-high rents concentrated in San Francisco specifically. None of that demand engine sits in East Palo Alto's condo tier. Here and across San Mateo County, higher rates plus growing attached-home inventory are doing the opposite: prices drifting sideways to down, and market times more than doubling.

What this means for your sale in one sentence: your home is an attached property competing in the slowest-moving segment of the local market during a week when rates just made every buyer's payment bigger, and that reality should drive both the pricing posture and the urgency of fixing the fixable problems at the property.

Square Footage, Bathrooms & HOA. The Answers

Square footage. You've told me the unit measures 1,580 sq ft. Two independent public-record sources say otherwise and agree with each other exactly: the San Mateo County Assessor's property characteristics (via Orange Coast Title's property profile) list 1,510 sq ft, and the CoreLogic tax record, pulled separately, also lists Building Sq Ft at 1,510 with the same figure repeated in its Realist-sourced "Primary SqFt" field. That second figure is the one the active MLS listing already uses: the listing's $/sq ft field is $601.91, and $908,888 divided by 1,510 comes out to $601.91 almost to the penny.

I could not find a documentary basis anywhere in the county or title record for 1,580. It's possible your 1,580 number includes an enclosed patio, a stairwell or landing counted differently, or simply reflects a builder's brochure figure from 1980 that was never reconciled with the assessor's measurement, which happens often in older HOA-era condo developments. Here's what I'd recommend: keep the county-verified 1,510 as the primary listed square footage, since that's what title, tax, and the MLS field all already agree on, and add a square footage disclosure noting that you believe the unit measures 1,580 sq ft, that the county assessor's record shows 1,510 sq ft, and that buyers should verify square footage to their own satisfaction, ideally with their own measurement, before removing contingencies. That's standard California practice for exactly this kind of gap and protects both of us going into escrow.

Bedrooms and bathrooms. The tax record actually already supports your 2.5-bath description; it was likely just read wrong. The county field is formatted "Baths (F/H): 2/1," meaning 2 full baths and 1 half bath, which is 2.5 baths, the same as what is marketed. Every other unit at Mission Palo Alto in the same title package shows the identical 2/1 configuration, which confirms 2.5 baths is the standard floor plan for this complex, not an anomaly for this unit.

HOA fee. The MLS listing showed $460 a year. That's been updated.

The Market Story

The strongest evidence for your value comes from inside your own gated community, right here in East Palo Alto. Two units in your complex (Mission Palo Alto is the community's name, not the city; both sales are East Palo Alto addresses a few doors from yours), each the smaller 1,280 sq ft floor plan against your 1,510, sold at $960,000 in May 2026 and $1,030,000 in April 2025. They prove what this community can command when a buyer connects with a unit.

You also already know what this year's market test looked like: two listing attempts this spring at $924,888 and $907,880 without a buyer. Combined with the feedback from this cycle's four showings (area, location, price point), the market has told us where the resistance sits, and it sits at or just below where the property is priced today.

One more data point matters, and it cuts against stretching the price: buyers cross-shopping this listing can find townhomes with a Palo Alto address at lower prices. The O'Keefe Townhouses on East O'Keefe Street, similar vintage, similar size, 1.5 baths instead of your 2.5, sold three times in the past 15 months between $712,500 and $765,000. Palo Alto and East Palo Alto are completely different markets, different schools, different reputation, and buyers know it the moment they look at a map, so those sales are not price anchors for your unit. But they are your competition for the same buyer's attention: when a similar-sized townhome with the more prestigious address is asking and clearing $150,000 to $195,000 less, asking a premium on the East Palo Alto side has to be justified by what your unit specifically offers, which is the larger floor plan, the extra full bath, and the gated community. That's a real argument, and it supports the current price. It does not support a higher one.

Add the backdrop from the front half of this report: East Palo Alto's attached-home market transacts 6 to 10 sales per year, county condo/townhome values are drifting down while market times double, and rates just jumped again this week. The honest read is that your value is real, the ceiling has been tested twice without a buyer, and the win comes from holding a defensible price while fixing presentation and perception, not from testing the ceiling a third time.

Pricing Strategy & Recommendation. An Independent Read

Setting the current listing aside and starting from the evidence alone: the same-complex sales at $960,000 and $1,030,000 are the proven high-water marks, but both closed before this year's rate climb, and the buyer math has moved since. At 6.75%, the payment on any given price is meaningfully heavier than it was when those two units sold, which means the same buyer pool now clears at lower prices. The East Palo Alto Woodland Creek sales at $760,000 to $775,000 set the floor for local attached product, your larger floor plan, extra half bath, and gated community justify a real premium over that floor, and this year's two withdrawn attempts at $908,000 to $925,000 mark where the market has already balked twice, in a softer rate environment than we have this week. Adjusting the same-complex evidence down for the rate move and the county-wide condo slide, the realistic clearing band today sits in the high $800s to low $900s.

Price below market: $850,000 to $875,000

Undercuts everything this property has asked in 2026 and creates the strongest possible urgency in a thin, 6-sales-a-year market. The trade is giving up potential upside for speed and certainty. This becomes the right move if the next three to four weeks pass without an offer, because in a market this thin, chasing the price down slowly costs more than repositioning once, decisively.

Price at market: $875,000 to $915,000 (where you are now)

The current $908,888 sits in the upper half of this band: above the EPA attached-home floor, below the same-complex sales, and at a $/sq ft below every comparable sale in this report. It is defensible, but with rates at 6.75% and county condo market times near a month, it needs the property-level frictions fixed and patience measured in weeks to get a fair test.

Price above market: $915,000+

Leans entirely on two same-complex sales that closed in a cheaper-money market, while ignoring the two failed attempts this spring, the 0.16-point rate jump this week, and the county-wide condo slide. I don't recommend it. A third unsuccessful test at a higher number would add weeks of market time to a listing whose days-on-market clock buyers are already watching.
