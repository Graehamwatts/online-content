The Market Story

East Palo Alto buyers have room to choose. As of October 8 there are 24 houses for sale, 8 of them already in contract, and the MLS counts 2.7 months of inventory. Over the last twelve months 87 homes sold at an average of $1,099,784, the same as the year before ($1,099,694). What changed is the pace, and who has the upper hand: the average home took 40 days to sell, up from 24, and the average sale closed at 98.7% of its original list price, down from 101.2%.

How a home was priced decided how it sold. Of the 78 east-of-101 sales with real time on the market, 28% closed over their original list price, in a median of 12 days, and 60% closed under it, after a median of 35 days. The median home closed at 97.8% of its original price.

A second home on the lot is a niche. In the last twelve months eleven east-of-101 sales had one. Nine of them are in the table below, and they closed between $1,065,000 and $1,425,000, a median of $1,250,000. The other two were a gated 5,920 sq ft estate with a 1,200 sq ft guest house, which sold for $2,110,000 in April, and a front-and-rear duplex that sold for $955,000 in October. The best of the updated or remodeled ones in the table was 2887 Fordham Street: two houses on 8,160 sq ft, a 4 bed, 2 bath in front and a 3 bed, 2 bath in back, seven bedrooms like yours. It sold for $1,425,000 in November after 94 days and a cut from $1,598,000. 2212 Dumbarton Avenue, a 4 bed, 3 bath house with an attached one-bedroom unit, sold for $1,379,000 in March. 1226 Laurel Avenue, a remodeled 3 bed, 2 bath with a 593 sq ft ADU, sold for $1,300,000 in September after 90 days.

The two second-home sales nearest you are smaller than your two homes together. 1017 Beech Street, 0.32 mile away, is a fully renovated house of 1,740 sq ft, the same size as your front house, with a studio and a finished garage unit. It sold for $1,200,000 in April, 12 days after it listed at $1,049,000. 434 Hibiscus Court, 0.30 mile away, a house of 1,310 sq ft with a separate unit, sold for $1,100,000 in June. 3 Shorebreeze Court, 0.15 mile from you, a 2003 house of 1,840 sq ft with no second unit, sold for $1,340,000.

Buyers pay for the second home mostly as extra space, and not much of it. Renovated older houses of 1,400 sq ft or more with no second unit sold for a median of $1,190,000 (six sales, $1,080,000 to $1,226,250). The four largest remodeled or updated second-unit sales, with 1,850 to 2,016 sq ft of total space, sold for $1,075,000 to $1,425,000, a median of $1,339,500. That puts the second home at about $150,000 over the baseline at the median and $235,000 at the top.

Among second-unit sales, only rebuilt or much larger properties have cleared $1.5 million. Of the 18 east-of-101 sales with a second unit in the last two years, only two sold above it: the 5,920 sq ft estate ($2,110,000) and 251 Gardenia Way, three new homes on one lot ($1,754,000). Your front house is getting a refresh, not a rebuild.

What you'd compete with today is mostly single-family. A buyer with $1.4 to $1.6 million can choose 2134 Capitol Avenue (4 bed, 3 bath, 1,720 sq ft) at $1,475,000, 1120 Alberni Street (5 bed, 3 bath, 2,485 sq ft, remodeled) at $1,375,000 after 64 days, or 1269 Runnymede Street (5 bed, 3 bath, 2,660 sq ft, built 2001) at $1,599,000. 2275 Pulgas Avenue (4 bed, 3 bath, 2,010 sq ft) went into contract at $1,500,000. The only other active listing with a new second house on the lot is 2260 Clarke Avenue, my listing: two homes on 12,045 sq ft, asking $1,599,000 since June 1 with no contract.

Rates are the headwind. The 30-year fixed was 7.50% on October 8, up from 6.36% a year ago. On a $1,462,500 purchase with 20% down, that is about $893 a month more for the same house.

Reading the numbers

Of the 78 East Palo Alto homes east of US-101 that sold in the last twelve months:

  • 22 homes (28%) priced it to sell and sold over their original ask, a median of $1,095,000 (about 5% over), in a median of just 12 days.
  • 9 homes (12%) sold at asking, a median of $1,050,000, in 19 days.
  • 47 homes (60%) overpriced and sold below their original ask. They started higher (median list $1,100,000) but sold for a median $1,065,000, about 5% under, and took a median 35 days, nearly 3 times as long.
  • 23 homes (29%) cut their price at least once before selling.
The overpriced group listed $106,056 higher and sold for $30,000 less. The first list price decided where each one ended up.
Why is the market pricing this way? Many sellers listed at the high end of what a neighbor had got, and buyers corrected them. 2887 Fordham listed at $1,598,000 and sold for $1,425,000 after 94 days. 2232 Euclid listed at $1,345,000 and sold for $1,200,000 after 148 days. Meanwhile buyers bid up 11 of the 33 homes that sold for $1.1 million or more, a median of 9.3% over their list price in 11 days: 1017 Beech listed at $1,049,000 and sold for $1,200,000, 164 Wisteria listed at $1,099,000 and sold for $1,226,250, and 2296 Pulgas listed at $1,235,000 and sold for $1,350,000. In this market buyers compete for a home that's priced within reach and wait out the rest.
What this all means. East Palo Alto buyers passed on list prices that reached and bid up the ones that sat a little under the comps. For your two homes, that points to listing under the price you hope to get and letting buyers compete for it. I recommend the mid-tier band below, $1,399,000 to $1,449,000. It sits on the best comparable sales, and reaching the expected midpoint from it takes a 0.9% to 4.5% jump over list. Homes first listed between $1.25 and $1.45 million show how that goes: three of the 16 closed over their list price, by 2.9% to 7.8%, and all three were newer community homes. The other 13 closed within 1% of list or below it, including every older house and two-home property in that list range, and the median of all 16 closed at 96.5% of list. A low list invites competition but doesn't promise it. A higher list puts you with the 18 homes at $1.1 million and up that sold under ask after a median of 33 days.

That's what I expect your two homes to sell for, not what I'd put on the sign. In East Palo Alto those are two different numbers, and the gap between them is the strategy.

How the expected sales price was built. The floor comes from real sales. The top is a judgment about what your extra space adds, and it's the less certain end. A price per square foot can't settle it: across the 29 updated and remodeled houses of 1,000 to 2,100 sq ft, square footage accounts for 8% of the price differences (r² of 0.08), and the fitted rate of $156 a square foot has a 95% range of -$55 to $367, which includes zero.

The back home: permit, final inspection and size. The county's land-use code for your parcel reads Single Family with ADU, which tells us the county knows about the back home. The City's rules for a second home. East Palo Alto's ADU ordinance doesn't allow an ADU to be sold separately from the main house, so your two homes sell as one property. It does allow renting the ADU on its own, with a 30-day minimum. The City's rent stabilization program treats a unit newly constructed with a certificate of occupancy on or after January 1, 1988 as partially exempt, and just-cause eviction rules still apply. A buyer who plans to rent will ask for the date of the back home's certificate of occupancy.
