This is a classic 1925 North of Panhandle two-flat with real scale, period character, and a remodeled owner's unit up top. It sits in one of San Francisco's most walkable, in-demand pockets, a short walk to the Panhandle, Divisadero's restaurants, and Golden Gate Park. The combination of an owner-occupied upper flat, a separate lower flat, and a two-car garage is exactly the configuration the market pays up for here.

Where the Market Is

San Francisco's NoPa and greater 94117 market has been steady to rising over the past year. The median sale price across all home types in 94117 was about $1,894,000 as of April 2026, up roughly 6.3% year over year, so values for well-located buildings like yours have been drifting up, not down. You bought in March 2023 at $2,365,000, near the bottom of the post-2022 reset, which has turned out to be good timing.

Two-unit buildings in this area are valued two ways at once: against other small multi-unit buildings, and against the income or owner-use they offer. Because you live in the upper unit and the lower flat is available to you, the cleaner read here is the comparable-sales approach, with the income simply as supporting upside rather than the main driver. The comps below point to a current value in the mid-$2.5M range, right in line with the independent Redfin estimate of $2,587,642.

There is one more layer worth understanding, and it works in your favor. A comparable-size single-family home in this neighborhood sells for materially more per square foot than a two-unit building. That gap is the condo-conversion or single-family-use opportunity, and it is covered in its own section below. You do not have to do anything with it, but it is real value sitting in the structure.

A Few Things Worth Noting

The lower unit as income

Since you live upstairs, this is supporting upside rather than the basis for value. If the lower flat were rented at market, a three-bedroom NoPa flat of that size would likely command somewhere in the range of $5,000 to $7,000 a month. That is a rough estimate to verify against current listings, but it shows the building can carry a meaningful share of its own costs if you ever want it to.

Condo-conversion optionality

As covered above, two-unit buildings are the most straightforward condo conversions in San Francisco, and your listing history flagged the potential. Conversion takes time, cost, and navigating the city's rules, so it is not a quick lever, but it is the path that would close the gap toward single-family pricing. Worth a conversation if it ever interests you.
