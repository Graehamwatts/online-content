The Market Story

Campbell townhome buyers have more choice than a year ago. At the end of September there were 34 townhomes for sale, double last year's 17 and nearly five times September 2023's 7. That is 4.9 months of supply against 2.4 a year ago. Closed sales have held up (76 through September against 80 last year; September isn't complete), so the extra homes are simply taking longer to sell.

Our team sold 503 Latimer Cir, a few doors from you, in February. It was a remodeled four-bedroom of 1,432 square feet: listed January 29 at $1,198,000, in contract in 6 days, closed February 20 at $1,311,110, 109% of the list price and $916 a square foot. Yours is a different house, one story and 1,013 square feet, so the dollars don't carry over. The pattern does. Three of the four updated or remodeled Fairmeadow homes drew bids: 556 Latimer closed at 117% of list, 538 Latimer at 127% and 503 at 109%. The fourth, 548 W Latimer, sold at its list price, and the dated 574 Latimer at 99.9%.

Your floor plan has sold six times since 2018, from $780,000 in 2019 to $1,101,000 in October 2023, when a cash buyer paid 110% of list for the unit with one of the largest yards. 570 Latimer Circle, the same plan, came on the market October 1 at $959,800. It is described as well maintained and move-in ready, and over the next two weeks it will tell us more about your price than any statistic.

Not every Campbell townhome gets bid up. Of the 79 resale townhouse sales in the last twelve months, 51% closed over the original list price. Under $1.1 million, where your home sits, only 31% did and 58% closed under it, at a median 98.0% of the original list after 32 days. Above $1.1 million, 67% closed over list in 9 days.

The market also changed in July. Townhomes that closed April through June went over list 73% of the time, at a median 104.3% in 8 days. Since July, 44% went over and 56% went under, at a median 97.6%. The 10 two-bedroom sales since July split evenly, with a median of 99.4% and 25 days.

A new community, Monarch, is selling two-bedroom, 2.5-bath townhomes less than a quarter mile away. Its 1,435 square foot plan went into contract at $1,155,790 for a closing next February, and builder prices are coming down: one 1,536 square foot plan has dropped $187,650 since April. That puts a ceiling on what a 1973 townhome of 1,013 square feet can ask.

Rates are the headwind. The 30-year fixed is 7.57% (Mortgage News Daily, October 2), up from 6.38% a year ago. On a $959,000 purchase with 20% down, that is $5,401 a month in principal and interest, $612 more than a year ago, before the $425 of HOA dues.

Reading the numbers

Of the 79 resale townhomes that sold in Campbell in the last twelve months:

  • 40 homes (51%) sold over their original ask, a median of 5% over, in a median of just 9 days.
  • 9 homes (11%) sold within 1% of ask, in a median 17 days.
  • 30 homes (38%) sold under their original ask, a median of 6% under, and took a median 44 days, about five times as long.
  • 16 homes (20%) cut their price, by a median 7.6%, and then took a median 52 days to sell, closing at 99.1% of their last price.
The homes that started high didn't win on price. They sold for less than the original ask and sat about five times longer than the ones that were priced to sell.
Why does your price range behave differently? Bidding wars in Campbell are a bigger-home story. Three- and four-bedroom townhomes selling above $1.1 million went over list 67% of the time; under $1.1 million it was 31%. At your price, buyers compare more alternatives, they have more time, and they pay attention to days on market. The ones that worked this year started clearly below value: 1381 Millich Ln listed at $899,000 and sold for $976,000, and 1358 Charmwood Sq listed at $899,000 and sold for $1,000,000, both in the spring.
What this means for you. Price with the market, not above it. At the Mid-tier band below, $949,000 to $979,000, you need a 1% to 4% jump over list to reach the middle of the expected range. Half of the two-bedroom townhomes sold since July closed over their original list, so that jump is reachable but not automatic, and the band keeps the home below the $1 million line where many searches stop.

Special Considerations

The HOA. Dues were $305 a month on your unit in 2022 and are $425 on the 2026 sales next door. At today's rates the extra $120 a month costs a buyer about $21,000 of purchase price, and every $100 of monthly dues is worth about $18,000. Buyers will ask for the budget, the reserve study and any planned assessments, so have them ready. The dues are also a selling point: they cover the roof, exterior paint, road maintenance, hazard insurance, a pool and a clubhouse.

The end-unit position. Your 2022 listing called out the end unit beside the greenbelt, and Fairmeadow buyers have paid for position. 538 Latimer, listed as the best location in the complex with an extra-large backyard, closed at 127% of its list price. 550 Latimer, with one of the largest yards, closed at 110%. Your yard, the greenbelt and the light belong in the first photos and the first line of the listing.

570 Latimer and timing. The same plan listed October 1 at $959,800 and is being shown now. If it sells quickly at or above its price, that supports the upper half of the Mid-tier band. If it sits, buyers are telling us this plan needs to start lower. Either way, we'll know in about two weeks, and a late-October listing still has about four weeks before Thanksgiving. If you don't need to sell this fall, a January launch is the alternative: the first quarter of 2026 went over list 53% of the time, although listings also build in the spring.

Preparing the home. A full remodel costs more than it adds. Each step up the ladder is worth about $50,000 to $65,000, and a kitchen and two baths cost more than that and take months. The better return is a cosmetic refresh: paint, flooring where it's worn, lighting, a deep clean and staging. Your own 2022 sale shows what that looks like: it listed with fresh paint and granite counters and closed 7.9% over list in six days.

New homes down the street. A buyer comparing your townhome at $959,000 with a new two-bedroom, 2.5-bath at $1,155,790 is saving almost $200,000 and getting a home they can move into now, in an established community with a pool, a garage and the greenbelt next door. The new home closes next February and carries a $351 to $400 HOA. That is your case to buyers.

The 1973 build. Buyers and their inspectors will look at the electrical panel, plumbing, windows and any textured ceilings. A pre-listing inspection and pest report give us the facts before the first showing, and they take the surprise out of negotiation.
