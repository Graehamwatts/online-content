The Market Story

Prices near you vary a lot block to block. The two homes on 2,500 sq ft lots east of you, both updated, sold for about $900,000: 590 Hurlingame Avenue at $900,000 in April and 528 Macarthur Avenue at $928,000 in August.

Homes on bigger lots in the Hoover Elementary area did better. 412 Stambaugh Street, a 3-bedroom sold as a project with room to improve on a 5,000 sq ft lot, brought $1,300,000 in May, and 30 Meadow Lane, a 2-bedroom of 890 sq ft on a 5,392 sq ft lot, sold for $1,325,000 in June, 147.6 percent of its asking price. One bigger-lot sale went the other way: 2921 Calvin Avenue, on a 5,000 sq ft lot in the Garfield Elementary area, took three cuts to sell at $875,000.

Toward downtown, small older homes did even better: 715 Roosevelt Avenue, a refreshed 2-bedroom bungalow, sold for $1,350,000, and 1145 Ebener Street, a 3-bedroom, for $1,400,000. Those blocks sit closer to downtown and are not a direct match.

In the numbers: across homes within about a mile of you, the typical home sold for 105.9 percent of its list price from July to September, offers came in about two weeks, and 79 percent of sales went over asking. The trailing three-month median was $1,710,000 in September, up from $1,592,500 a year earlier, and months of inventory were 1.3, far under the 3 months of a balanced market.

Since early 2024, price per square foot for homes of your size around here is up roughly 7 to 10 percent, depending on the months you compare. The rest of the gap between your purchase price and today's value comes from where you started, a fixer on a corner lot bought at a fixer's price, and from any work you have put in since.
