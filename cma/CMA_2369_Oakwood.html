2369 Oakwood is a compact, well-kept two-bedroom cottage on a generous 4,200 square foot lot in the Palo Alto Park pocket of East Palo Alto, east of US-101. For a home this size, the attached two-car garage, central cooling, and the size of the lot are real advantages. The lot in particular is the story here: at 4,200 square feet with only 720 square feet built, there is genuine room to expand or add an ADU, which is exactly what a chunk of this buyer pool is looking for.

Where the Market Is. And What That Means for You

East Palo Alto's single-family market has firmed up over the last year, and homes are clearing at or above asking nearly every month when they are priced right. You are bringing 2369 Oakwood to market in late spring, the strongest demand window of the year on the Peninsula, which works in your favor.

For a 720 square foot two-bedroom, the right comp set is the small east-of-101 cottages, the 2-bed and 3-bed homes under about 1,100 square feet. Over the last six months those have sold in a band of roughly $790,000 to $1,035,000, with the honestly priced two-bed cottages clustering around $860,000 to $925,000. Your independent valuation came in at $887,200, right in the middle of that cluster. The comps and the AVM line up.

The buyer for this home is a first-time buyer, a downsizer, or an investor, and that pool is active but price-sensitive. The competition right now is a set of similar small homes priced from the high $700,000s to the high $900,000s, several of which have been sitting because they reached too high. That is the single most important lesson for your pricing, and the next sections show exactly how it has played out.

Mortgage rates sit around 6.5% today, down from the 7%-plus peaks of 2023 but well above the 2021 lows. Entry-level demand in East Palo Alto has held up because this is one of the few Peninsula zip codes where a buyer can still get into a detached home with a yard under $1M. That keeps your buyer pool active. The plan is simple: price 2369 Oakwood where the small-cottage comps actually cleared, lean on the spring demand, and let the expansion potential of the lot pull in the add-value buyers.

Three Paths Forward. The Real Trade-Off

Because this is an investment property rather than a residence, the decision is about what you want this capital to do next. Three honest paths:

Path A. Sell now, take the gain, redeploy (recommended if your goal is to exit)

Realistic clearing range: $879,000 to $929,000. The spring market is strong and the small-home buyer pool is active. List in the competitive band, market the lot and expansion potential hard, and aim for a two-to-four week sale at or over asking. Delivering the home vacant and clean (see the occupancy note) will widen the buyer pool and help the top of the range.

Path B. Light prep, then list for the top of the range.

Realistic clearing range: $915,000 to $965,000. The home is already in good condition, so prep here is light and high-return: a deep clean, fresh neutral paint where needed, simple staging to make 720 square feet feel larger, and strong photography that sells the lot and the garage. For a small home, presentation moves the needle a lot relative to its cost. The trade is a modest budget and one to two extra weeks.

Path C. Keep it as a rental. For a long-term holder.

You already hold this as an investment, so continuing is a real option. A clean two-bed cottage with a garage in this area rents in the range of roughly $3,000 to $3,600 a month. Against a low Proposition 13 tax basis, the carry is easy, and the real long-game here is the lot: adding an ADU could create a second income stream and meaningfully lift the value. The case for selling instead is that the equity is large relative to the current rent, and that capital may work harder elsewhere. This is a portfolio decision.

Notes & Caveats
Occupancy and showing access. Tax records show an absentee owner, so the home is most likely tenant-occupied. Before listing, confirm whether a tenant is in place and the terms of any lease. A tenant-occupied home limits showing access and can narrow the buyer pool, and California rules govern notice and relocation if a tenant must move. The cleanest path to the top of the range is to deliver the home vacant and clean. If a lease runs past a likely close date, that needs to be factored into both timing and price.

The lot is a real value lever

At 4,200 square feet with only 720 square feet built, this lot has room for an ADU or an expansion. A meaningful slice of the East Palo Alto buyer pool is specifically hunting for small homes on usable lots for exactly this reason. Marketing the expansion and ADU potential, not just the existing cottage, is how you reach those buyers and support the upper end of the range.

Capital gains. This is an investment property.

The last recorded sale was $425,000 in 2015, so a sale near $900,000 implies a large taxable gain. Because the home is not your primary residence, the IRS Section 121 exclusion does not apply. The gain would be taxable, and any depreciation taken is subject to recapture. A 1031 exchange into another investment property is the main tool to defer that tax if you plan to redeploy. Talk to your CPA before deciding, since the tax treatment can change which path makes sense.
