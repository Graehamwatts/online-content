The Market Story

Two houses with your exact floor plan sold this summer. 4839 Snow Drive, just down the street, is the same 3-bed, 2-bath, 1,056 square foot home built in 1962. It sold on July 8 for $840,000 after 19 days, marketed as needing some updating. 617 Savstrom Way, the same model on a 7,124 square foot corner lot, sold on July 24 for $900,000. That one opened at $998,000 and took 64 days to find a buyer.

Updated versions of these homes sell well above that. 5083 Roeder Road, 1,085 square feet with an updated kitchen, listed at $899,999 and went into contract four days later; it closed at $1,056,000. 597 Oldbrook Court and 4435 Concerto Drive, both updated or remodeled, closed at $1,015,000 and $1,050,000. On these streets, condition has been worth roughly $150,000 to $215,000.

Your lot is the wild card. County records show 11,200 square feet, about double the 5,000 to 6,000 square feet most homes in your tract sit on. The corner lot on Savstrom brought $60,000 more than the standard lot on Snow, though condition played a part too. Extra land matters most to a buyer who wants room for an ADU or a big addition.

Within about a mile of you, homes are selling for about 102 percent of their final list price, and the typical one goes under contract in about two weeks. There's roughly half a month of inventory, far under the three months that marks a balanced market. That's a seller's market. The three-month median sale price was $1,165,000 in August on 36 sales, and it has ranged from $1,110,000 to $1,391,000 over the past three years as the mix of homes selling shifted.

For you, that means steady demand. Well-priced homes near you still draw competition, even with rates above 7 percent. 5083 Roeder closing $156,000 over its list price in July says as much.
