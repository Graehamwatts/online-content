Where the Market Is

925 Dorel came on the market September 9 at $2,300,000 and was cut $100,001 on September 29 to $2,199,999. It has had two open houses, no offer date has been posted, and it has not gone pending. The one price move so far was down.

The nearest sales set the price. Six Updated and Remodel homes in the foothill pocket closed since April, within 0.9 mile of Dorel on nearby foothill streets, for $1,899,888 to $2,410,000. They sold for a median of $932 a square foot, which is $2,076,000 for 2,227 square feet. Across all 13 Updated and Remodel sales in the foothill and Piedmont Hills neighborhoods within two miles, the median is $971 a square foot, or $2,163,000. Dorel's list works out to $988. That is above both medians, and those homes were finished and had central air.

The closest sale of all is 3645 El Grande Drive, 0.15 mile away, which closed in February at $2,627,000. It went to a bidding war in a hotter market, had over $800,000 of upgrades, a 15,943 square foot lot and 2,550 square feet. It shows what the street can do in a hot market, and it is not the target. The target is what a buyer pays in October for a house with this finish level, this condition and these questions.

The market softened over the summer. Within two miles, sale price to original list averaged 104.1% in the first quarter and 103.6% in the second, then 98.3% in July and August (weighted by the number of sales). The 31 September closings, which may still be entered, came back to 103.2% in 20 days, so it is not a straight line. Homes for sale reached 52 in September against 19 a year ago. Of the 13 Updated and Remodel sales, 9 closed over their original list price. The median sale was 104.9% of original list in 10 days. Four did not. Two of those listed above their comps and sat: 1091 Colette Drive took 49 days and sold at 86.8% of its original list, and 3211 Lenark Drive took 33 days and sold at 96.8%.

The house down the street tells the same story from the other side. 861 Dorel Drive listed in May at $2,297,000. It is now $1,875,000 after 146 days and a $422,000 cut. The sellers listed it at the best case price, and it has not sold in 146 days.

Worked out in two steps. First, what a finished 2,227 square foot Updated or Remodel home sells for here: the six foothill sales give $2,076,000, all 13 give $2,163,000, and a size fit across the 13 gives $2,139,000. That is $2,080,000 to $2,160,000, just below the list price. Second, what is different about this house. It has no central air (all 13 comps do), older systems, and open questions on permits and square footage. I adjusted for those, with smaller adjustments at the top of the range. If the living area turns out 5% smaller than the county says, the range drops to $1,942,000 to $2,097,000.

How sure am I? The range depends on which sales count as comparable. If I widened the set to every Updated and Remodel sale of this size within two miles, including Hillcrest, the East Foothills and Glen Firth Drive, 21 sales give a median of $917 a square foot and an expected range of $1,930,000 to $2,030,000. I do not think those neighborhoods set the price for Dorel, but the gap is real. It is the reason I would open below the range and keep Path C for clean answers only.

Three Paths Forward. Offer Strategy

I do not know yet whether other offers are in, so the plan starts with a call to Bryan. The list is above the range and the seller has already cut once, which gives us room to negotiate. What matters before we write is a pre-approval letter at the offer price. At the top of Path C that means about $425,000 down and a loan of $1,700,000.

Path A. Under the range, clean and conditioned (recommended)
$1,975,000
Offer with a 7 to 10 day inspection contingency, a request for the permit papers and a measured floor plan, and a seller credit or repair for the items the inspections turn up. It sits about 5% under the midpoint, which the findings above support. If the seller counters, meeting around $2,025,000 still lands inside the range.
Path B. Lower half of the range
$2,025,000 to $2,075,000
If another offer shows up before we are in contract. At $2,050,000 you would still keep the inspection, title and appraisal protection, and I would hold the short window only after the inspections are done.
Path C. Stretch
$2,100,000 to $2,125,000
Only if the permit and square footage answers come back clean, the insurance quotes work, and you want this house more than the next one. If you give up appraisal protection, decide in advance how big a gap you will cover. $2,125,000 is about the top of the range after repairs, and above it if the wider set of sales described above is the better guide. I would not pay more for this house as it stands.

One more way to look at it. The same price buys more in the foothill pocket than it does in the Piedmont Hills subdivisions a mile or more to the west, which are somewhat newer, mostly on smaller lots, and sold for about 6% more per square foot. Dorel's advantages are the lot, the views and a kitchen and baths the listing calls high-end. Its disadvantages are in the adjustments above, and each one has a price.

Where This Leaves You

925 Dorel is a good house in a good spot, with a kitchen and baths the listing calls high-end. It is priced like a finished remodel, and it is not one yet. The systems are older, a few permit and square footage questions are open, and the market has moved toward buyers since the summer. $2,020,000 to $2,130,000 is what I think it clears at. Then we write $1,975,000 with the protections above. If another offer shows up before we are in contract, we move to Path B, and $2,125,000 is the stop.
