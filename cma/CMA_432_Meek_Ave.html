Why Brand-New Doesn't Change The Ceiling More Than This

This is the part worth sitting with, because it is not intuitive. You spent real money building three brand-new homes, so it feels like that should be worth more than what a 60-year-old triplex would fetch. For a family buying a home to live in, it would be. For the buyer pool that actually buys a one-parcel triplex, an investor, it mostly is not, and here is exactly why.

To be fair to the other side of this: being brand new does add real value, and it is not nothing. Some investors specifically look for new construction, precisely because it means no near-term roof, plumbing, or electrical surprises, and that is a genuine selling point worth marketing hard. There is a trade-off, and it is one of the biggest factors in how an income buyer actually decides: the price you need to charge for that newness directly lowers the cap rate, and cap rate is one of the top things investors compare deal to deal. At the top of the revised range, an investor is looking at roughly a 3.9 percent cap. I checked what real, recently-closed Hayward triplexes and fourplexes are actually trading at rather than assume a number, and it turns out that is right in line with them, not thin by comparison. That is a meaningfully different picture than the prior draft of this analysis assumed, and it is covered in full in the cap rate section below.

This is genuinely not a settled, one-way argument, it depends on the buyer. Some will pay up for low maintenance risk and take a lower yield for the peace of mind. Others will always take the higher cap rate on an older building and self-insure the repair risk. I am not going to tell you it is definitively one or the other, because it is not, that is exactly why the data below matters more than my opinion of it: look at the actual comparable sales and judge for yourself where the market has really landed. What the sales record does confirm is the gap between what a family buyer would pay for one of these homes on its own (see the individual-home comps further down, they are meaningfully higher per door) and what an investor pays for all three together as a single income asset. That gap is exactly why the individual-sale path is still worth several hundred thousand dollars more, and exactly why, since the city's cost took that path off the table for now, the as-is ceiling lands where the comps below show it.
