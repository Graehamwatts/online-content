This home has two things that genuinely set it apart in El Sobrante: a fourth bedroom and a third full bathroom. Of the fifteen comparable homes that sold recently, every single one had only two baths. Built in 1985, it is also newer than most of the neighborhood's housing stock, and the 10,400 square foot lot is comfortably above average.

The Market Story

Here is the honest picture. Over the last six months, fifteen single-family homes in the 1,450 to 1,950 square foot range sold in El Sobrante. The middle of that group closed at about $765,000, and the typical home took a little over two weeks to find a buyer. It is a steady market, not a frenzy, but well-presented homes are still moving.

Where this home fits is the interesting part. Most of what sold is a three-bedroom, two-bath home, often from the 1950s or 60s. This one is a four-bedroom with three full baths, built in 1985. That combination is rare here and it matters to buyers who need the extra room and the extra bathroom. The closest match on size that recently sold is 5466 Cabrillo Sur, a 1,714 square foot home of similar age that closed at $850,000, and that home had fewer bedrooms, fewer baths, and a smaller lot than this one.

The recent sales spanned a wide range, from the mid $400s to just over $900,000. The low end was older or smaller homes that needed work; the top end, like 2164 La Mirada at $905,000, were homes that showed well. That spread tells you condition is the single biggest swing factor on the final number.

Putting it together, this home today most likely lands in the mid to high $800,000s, with room toward $900,000 if it presents in strong, updated condition. For context, the county shows it last changed hands in 1986 for $166,000, so the equity built up over roughly four decades here is substantial.

This is a snapshot, not a sales pitch. The point is to give a real, current read on value backed by actual sales, so any decision down the road starts from solid ground.

Pricing Strategy Analysis

If a sale were ever on the table, here is how the three approaches would likely play out, based on what these sales actually did.

Price Below Market

List slightly under the comps to drive competition. With several homes here selling in under two weeks, this can pull multiple offers and land at or above market. Works best when the home shows well and you want a fast, clean sale.

Price At Market

List right where the comparable sales support, in the mid-to-high $800,000s given the extra bed, third bath, and larger lot. Attracts serious buyers and tends to sell at a healthy pace. The balanced approach.

Price Above Market

Push above the comps and bet on condition carrying a premium, like La Mirada did at $560/sqft. The risk is real: homes that started too high, like 5774 Friar Ct at 117 days, sat. Extra time on market usually costs you.

Things Worth Noting

The 3-bath, 4-bed advantage

Every one of the 15 recent comps had only two bathrooms. A third full bath plus a fourth bedroom is a real, marketable edge that justifies pricing above the typical El Sobrante comp.

Newer construction

Built in 1985, the home is newer than most of the area's housing stock (many comps date to the 1930s through 60s). That generally means newer systems and fewer deferred-maintenance surprises.

Condition is the swing factor

Recent sales ran from $291 to $560 per square foot almost entirely on condition. A walkthrough to assess updates versus original finishes would narrow the estimate meaningfully.
