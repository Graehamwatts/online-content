Where The Market Is

It is hard to believe it has been over a decade since I handed you the keys to this home. Since then, East Palo Alto has kept building a real track record, and your specific pocket of it, the University Square community, has held up especially well.

Twenty-two single-family homes in the 2 to 4 bedroom, 1,500 to 2,600 sq ft range sold in East Palo Alto proper over the trailing 12 months. I personally listed 6 of those 22: 909 Baines Street, 3 Shorebreeze Court, 2271 Euclid Avenue, 828 Weeks Street, 2247 Menalto Avenue, and 2495 Gloria Way. That is roughly a quarter of all the activity in this exact size band this year running through my own hands, so this read on the market is not a general one, it is built on deals I was in the room for.

I pulled comps specifically from MLS Area 322, East of U.S. 101 in East Palo Alto, rather than by zip code. Your zip, 94303, also covers ultra-luxury Palo Alto proper, and mixing those two very different markets together would badly skew any number I gave you.

Inside that area, homes built in the same 2000-2001 era as yours, especially the ones in University Square itself, are trading at a real premium over similar-size, similar-age homes just outside the complex. 928 Wilks Street and 939 Oakes Street, both in your complex, cleared $783 and $809 a square foot. 909 Baines Street, the exact same 2,020 sq ft floorplan as your home but outside University Square, cleared $669 a square foot. That gap is the complex premium, and it is a real, measurable part of your value.

Newer-construction East Palo Alto homes built after 2009, in communities like Montage and Villa Tuscana, sold in a similar $1.35 million to $1.44 million range this year, but they are a different construction era and finish level than your home. I am including them below only as market context, not as direct comps.

The one comp with full original-list detail on file, 956 Mouton Circle, listed at $1,368,000 on 10/24/2025 and closed nine days later at $1,475,000, $107,000 over its original ask, no price cuts needed. That single data point lines up with the broader pattern across your closest comps: fast sales, no reductions, and pricing that held or beat the original ask.

Why is the market pricing this way? University Square and the similar 2000-2001 era complexes around it are a small, well-defined pocket of inventory. There simply are not many homes like yours coming up for sale at once, so when one does, buyers who want that floorplan and that HOA community move on it quickly rather than waiting for a better deal to appear.

What this all means. None of your closest comps needed a price cut. If you ever decide to explore a sale down the road, that same discipline, pricing at what the data actually supports rather than reaching for a number, is what has produced the fastest, cleanest outcomes in your complex.
