The Market Story

The best read on your home is the building next door. 1839 15th Street sits 0.06 miles from you, was built the same year, and two of its one-bedroom condos sold recently. Unit 253 (756 sq ft) closed at $690,000 in October 2025. Unit 455 (753 sq ft) closed at $700,000 on August 27, 2026, after 21 days on the market. That is almost exactly your size.

Other one-bedrooms within about half a mile ran from $510,000 for a recently remodeled unit on South Van Ness to $780,000 for a newer building in Hayes Valley with a den. The group's median was $640,000 and $812 per square foot. Condos in your corner of the Mission closed higher than the group median, because location and the 15th Street complex pull buyers.

Pricing mattered. The condos that sat were the ones that started high: 68 McCoppin Street took 144 days at $699,000, 140 South Van Ness #417 took 141 days, and 1875 Mission Street #304 took 114 days. Units that opened near the market, like 8 Buchanan and 550 South Van Ness, were gone in under three weeks.

Across the wider Mission, Hayes Valley and SoMa area, the three-month median condo price rose from about $970,000 in January to $1,156,000 in September, and buyers are now paying about 105 to 113 percent of the list price. The closest one-bedrooms behave more modestly, closing at about 98 percent of their original list price. There are about 2.6 months of inventory, below the three months that marks a balanced market.

For you, that means demand is healthy and getting stronger, and your building has recent sales to point to. Today's value sits below your 2019 purchase price because one-bedroom condos in the city peaked around then.
