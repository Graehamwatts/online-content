The Market Story

Start with your own street. 2620 Fordham, about a block from you with nearly your floor plan (3 bedrooms, 2 baths, 1,160 square feet), came on the market in August at $1,200,000. It has been cut twice, to $1,098,000, and it's still for sale after six weeks. Buyers are telling that seller the number is lower.

The closest match that actually sold is 1403 Kavanaugh Drive, half a mile away. Same 1,150 square feet, 3 bedrooms and 2 baths, updated with fresh paint and new flooring. It sold for $990,000 in June, 19 days after it listed. At the other end, 2543 Hazelwood Way, a 3-bed, 2-bath of 1,100 square feet marketed on its potential, closed on September 24 at $820,000. Updated 3-bed, 2-bath homes within a mile of you have sold between $943,000 and $1,065,000 since June.

A full remodel moves the number a lot further. 2551 Emmett Way, a couple of blocks from you, sold on September 22 for $1,276,000 after a top-to-bottom remodel. That's $456,000 more than the Hazelwood sale for a house of the same 1,100 square feet. Condition is the biggest swing factor on your side of town.

Across East Palo Alto, single-family homes are selling for about 99 percent of their final list price, and the typical one takes about 23 days to go under contract. In mid-2024 buyers were paying 105 to 106 percent of list and homes moved in about 11 days. Supply has loosened a little too: about two months of inventory in August 2026, against an average of about 1.3 months from September 2024 to August 2025. Anything under three months still leans toward sellers, just less than it did. Through all of it, the city's three-month median sale price has stayed between $946,000 and $1,180,000 for three years. In August it was $1,035,000.

For you, that adds up to steady value. Prices have held in the same band. What changed is how long buyers take and how hard they push back on a home priced above the recent sales, like the one down your street.
