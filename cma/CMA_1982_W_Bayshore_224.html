My honest read: updates help, but they are not what is driving the big dollar swings in this building. #310, the one comp explicitly marketed as "tastefully updated," is listed at $700.37 a square foot. That is meaningfully below #115 ($741.19/sqft) and #230 ($737.88/sqft), the two strongest performers in the entire comp set, neither of which leans on an "updated" pitch, they sell on floor and view instead. In a small 1-bedroom condo like this, the data says buyers are paying more for which way the unit faces and what floor it is on than for how new the kitchen looks. I want to flag the honest limit here too: I do not have confirmed update-status on every comp, only #310 explicitly says it, so this is directionally supportive, not proof beyond doubt. But it lines up with what I would expect in a building like this, cosmetic updates shorten your time on market and help photos and showings, they just are not the lever that explains a $50,000-plus swing the way a creek view does.

If it helps for how to frame this with a seller who has updated their unit: the honest, gentle version is "the updates absolutely help you sell faster and show better, buyers will notice and appreciate it, but the data in this building says the real price driver is the view, not the finish level, so I would not price purely on the renovation." That keeps the update as a genuine asset in the marketing story without setting an unrealistic price expectation around it.
