Where the Market Is

6628 Chabot came on the market October 2 at $1,895,000. In Rockridge and North Oakland the list price is where the bidding starts. There were 34 single family sales in Oakland and Berkeley within a mile, 1,400 to 2,800 square feet, closed since mid April (6565 Chabot Road, which went under contract the day it listed, is shown but not counted). Of those, 33 closed over their original list price, and 26 closed at 120% of list or more. The median was 137% of list, in 13 days. Across all of zip 94618, sale price to list price averaged 128% from January through September, against 116% over the same months of 2025 and 117% in 2024. Homes sold in 16 days on average from March through September, against 21 a year ago, and there were 0.4 months of inventory in July.

That would put this house near $2.5 million if the whole zip code were the guide. I don't think it is, because of how this house was priced. The twelve closest sales listed at $568 to $995 a square foot and sold at $849 to $1,211, a median of $1,004. The homes that listed lowest gained the most. 5451 Lawton listed at $1,195,000 and sold at $2,200,000, and 5360 James listed at $975,000 and sold at $1,900,000. Chabot lists at $995 a square foot, which is already at the top of where those sales began. Buyers converge on about $1,000 to $1,250 a square foot for a finished Oakland home in this pocket, so a house that starts at $995 climbs less in percentage terms. My midpoint is $2,250,000, or 19% over list. The pocket median shows 37%.

6212 Rockwell, listed at the same $1,895,000 on September 16, went pending on September 30. It is the best single guide to this house: the list price is the same, the size is close (2,067 sq ft against 1,904) and it is 0.3 mile away. It hasn't closed, so its price isn't public yet. When it records, I'll update this report.

One more reason not to lean on the highest ratios: the bidding has not started yet. The 12 closest sales took 6 to 23 days to reach a contract. The one house on this street that sold early, 6565 Chabot Road, went under contract the day it listed, August 23, at $1,725,000 against a list of $1,295,000, and closed September 10. That is 33% over list, close to the pocket median, so the early offer was a full price.

Market Trend Context

Monthly statistics for single family homes in zip code 94618, which is Rockridge and the neighborhoods around it. Source: MLSListings Matrix Stats, January 2023 through September 2026. Monthly counts are small (2 to 19 sales), so single months swing with the mix of homes that closed. September is the newest month and a few late closings may still be entered.

Average sale price, monthly. January to September 2026 averaged $2,111,000 across 85 sales, 4.0% above the same months of 2025 (weighted by the number of sales).
Sale price as a share of the list price, monthly. It stayed between 110% and 125% in 28 of the 36 months of 2023 to 2025. In 2026 it has been above 120% in every month since March (122% to 137%), and it was 133.7% in September.
Average days to sell, monthly. 13 to 21 days from March to September 2026. The long months in winter are mostly thin sales.
Months of inventory. Seven of the nine months of 2026 were under 1.5, which means the homes for sale would sell out in about six weeks or less at that month's pace. It rose to 2.0 in September because the number of homes for sale roughly doubled while sales held at six, against 7.3 in September 2025 (a month with four sales).

Prices are above last year, sale to list ratios have been above 120% for seven straight months, which has not happened before in the three year record, and homes sell in two to three weeks. September's average price per square foot, $1,041, is about 6% above the March to August average of $985, on six sales. I don't read that as a trend, and I wouldn't discount the fall for seasonality either: Rockridge buyers kept paying in October in 2023, 2024 and 2025, with ratios of 116%, 114% and 126%.

For this set, the three land within $10,000 of each other. I rounded the average to $2,250,000 and gave it a range of plus or minus 5.5%, which is the width I use for a one of a kind house. The top of that range, $2,375,000, is $1,247 a square foot. The highest price per square foot for an updated or remodeled Oakland home in this set is $1,247, so an offer above the range would pay more per foot than any sale I found in Oakland since April.

How sure I am. Less than a tight range might suggest. The twelve closest sales vary a lot: size adjusted, they run from $1,673,000 to $2,409,000, and only 1 of the 12 adjusted above my midpoint. The three checks lean on the same few sales. Without 5739 Presley Way they would land nearer $2,150,000, and without 5501 Thomas Check 3 falls to $2,127,000. The accessory unit question cuts both ways: with 5956 Canning Street and the three accessory unit sales all in, my midpoint is about $2,300,000, and with all four out it is about $2,200,000. So treat $2,125,000 to $2,375,000 as the middle of the likely outcomes. A house this unusual could reasonably sell anywhere from about $1,900,000 (the typical home) to $2,650,000 (about the top sale in the set, 5401 Belgrave at $2,655,000). If I used the whole zip code's July to September average of 134% of list, the price would be $2,546,000. I don't think that's the right guide, because those ratios include homes listed at $570 to $800 a square foot, but it is the case to keep in mind if bidding gets heated. The other direction is the typical home at $1,912,000. That is what this house would sell for if a buyer treated it as an ordinary updated 1,904 square foot home, and I don't expect that.
