The Market Story

East Palo Alto's 94303 zip actually covers two very different markets, the affordable East Palo Alto side you're in, and ultra-luxury Palo Alto proper on the other side of the freeway. Every number in this report is filtered specifically to East Palo Alto, not the zip code as a whole, so what you're reading here is your real competitive set.

I individually verified twelve sold homes in East Palo Alto over roughly the past nine months, sized 960 to 1,550 square feet, and pulled the original list price, sold price, and days on market for every one of them. The headline number is not what you might expect from a hot Bay Area headline: the median home in this cohort closed at exactly 100.0% of its original asking price. This is not a market where everything sells over ask by default. It rewards realistic pricing and punishes reaching, and the data below shows both sides of that clearly.

Condition explains most of the spread. Verified sold $/sq ft in this cohort ran from $774 for a fixer to $1,216 for a full remodel with permits. Your home, with its 2019 kitchen and bath work now aging and unconfirmed since, sits closer to the middle of that range than either extreme.

One more thing worth naming directly: this home sold for $1,200,000 in 2019, and the $1,100,000 to $1,250,000 range above puts it at or above that number today. The pocket's fixer and dated-condition sales are now running well above what this home fetched in 2019 on a $/sq ft basis, and the homes commanding real premiums today are the ones with fresh, recent finish work, plus the larger lot and cul-de-sac location this home carries. The market moved up, and this home is priced to move up with it, not just hold its 2019 position.

Reading the numbers

Of the 12 comparable East Palo Alto homes that sold in the last several months:

  • 5 homes (41.7%) priced it to sell and sold over their original ask, a median of $1,130,000 (about 9.3% over asking), in a median of 15 days.
  • 3 homes (25%) priced at market and sold within 1% of asking, in a median of just 11 days, the fastest group of the three.
  • 4 homes (33.3%) overpriced and sold below their original ask. They started highest of the three groups (median list $1,228,500) but sold for a median of $1,190,000, about 3.1% under, and took 43 days, nearly 3x as long as either disciplined group.
  • 3 of the 12 (25%) needed at least one price cut before selling: 1523 Ursula Way, 2271 Euclid Avenue, and 2383 Menalto Avenue.
The overpriced group's homes sold for a median of $60,000 less than the priced-to-sell group's, despite starting with a higher asking price. Reaching on the list price bought them nothing but 3x the wait.
Why is the market pricing this way? Unlike some hotter Bay Area pockets right now, East Palo Alto's entry-level tier is not automatically rewarding every seller with a bidding war, the exact 100.0% median list-to-sale ratio says that plainly. What separates the winners from the losers here is realistic, comp-backed pricing on day one. The disciplined third of sellers who priced at or under a defensible number got bid up fast. The third who reached corrected downward over six-plus weeks and still landed below their own ask. This is a pricing-discipline market, not a frenzy market, and that changes the strategy: sharp pricing here is not just about speed, it is about which side of a real dollar gap you end up on.
What this all means. The market punishes optimistic anchoring and rewards realistic, comp-backed pricing, not through speed alone but through final price. That is the case for the Recommended tier below, and it is why the suggested list sits at or under the value the comps actually support rather than stretching for a number the data does not back. Listing in the range the closest verified comps support puts you in the roughly 42% majority that gets bid up. Listing above it risks the 33% minority that cuts and settles under its own ask.
