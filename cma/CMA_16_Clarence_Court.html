Reading the numbers

Of the 12 comparable East Palo Alto homes that sold in the last twelve months:

  • 5 homes (42%) priced it to sell and sold over their original ask, a median of $1,125,000 (about 6% over asking), in a median of just 10 days.
  • 3 homes (25%) priced at market and sold right at asking, in about 11 days.
  • 4 homes (33%) overpriced and sold below their original ask. They started as the highest-priced group (median list $1,187,000) but sold for a median of $1,160,000, about 2% under, and took 43 days, more than four times as long as the priced-to-sell group.
  • Of the twelve, 2 homes (17%) needed at least one price cut before selling: 1523 Ursula Way cut $30,000, 215 Wisteria Drive cut $103,000 and still closed under even the reduced price.
The overpriced group took over 4 times as long to sell and still closed under their own number. The priced-to-sell group did not wait around to find out, they closed in 10 days at a premium.
Why is the market pricing this way? This looks like a straightforward anchoring gap. The sellers in the overpriced bucket started with the highest list prices in the cohort, likely anchored to an optimistic number, a neighbor's headline sale or a Zestimate, rather than what the actual comp set supported. The market corrected them over several weeks of soft activity, and they ultimately sold below where they started. The 67% majority who priced at or near a defensible number got bid up or cleared at asking instead, and did it fast. The correlation between price positioning and speed here is real and fairly strong (r = -0.63): pricing right in this market does not just mean a better final number, it means a dramatically shorter process.
What this all means. The market punishes optimistic anchoring and rewards realistic, comp-backed pricing, both in speed and in final price. That is the whole case for the Recommended tier below, and it is why the list price suggested there sits at the value the comps support rather than above it. Listing in the range the closest verified comps actually support puts you in the 67% majority that clears at or above asking, fast. Listing above it puts you in the 33% that cuts and settles under its own ask, more than four times slower.
