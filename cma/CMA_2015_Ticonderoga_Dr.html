Why this analysis exists. You're deciding whether to sell this house as it is, put a modest amount into it first, or take on a heavier renovation. Each of those puts it in a different tier of the Highlands market, and buyers here pay very differently for each tier. Below is what each path is worth today, what it costs, how long it takes, and what it leaves in your pocket, measured against the 31 single-family sales that closed in the Highlands between October 2025 and September 2026.
The Market Story

Thirty-one single-family homes closed in the San Mateo Highlands in the last twelve months, from $2,300,000 for a dated Eichler to $4,387,500 for a 2022 new build. Today exactly one house is for sale in the whole neighborhood.

Among Eichlers close to your size, price follows condition almost entirely. The dated homes, sold largely the way long-time owners left them, closed between $2,300,000 and $2,620,000. Updated homes, with newer kitchens, baths or systems, went for $2,620,000 to $2,930,000, and full remodels for $2,860,000 to $3,250,000. They sit on the same streets and feed the same schools. What separates them is the work done inside.

Your home sits in the dated group today: original paneling, laminate floors, Formica counters, two dated baths, and no heat you can count on. What sets it apart is the land and the extras. At 12,638 square feet on a cul-de-sac, it has one of the biggest lots of any Eichler in this report, the sewer lateral is already done, and the new studio out back gives a buyer the office or art studio that the neighbors don't have.

Buyers here are still competing. Of the 29 sales that had real time on the market, 79% closed over their original asking price, at a median of 109.4% of list, in a median of 9 days. The headwind is rates. The 30-year fixed crossed 7% this week for the first time since January 2025, a week after the Fed raised rates for the first time since 2023. Every sale in this report went into contract before that jump, so the ranges below sit roughly 10 to 14 percent under what each tier's sales brought this year, on purpose. They're set for this fall's buyers.

What this actually says. Condition sets the price here, not size. Inside the dated tier, square footage explains almost none of the difference in price (r² 0.01): a 1,540 sq ft home and an 1,840 sq ft home both sold for $2,500,000. The tier medians are $2,437,500 dated, $2,725,000 updated and $3,010,000 remodeled. Each step up is worth about the same: roughly $290,000 from dated to updated, and $285,000 more from updated to fully remodeled. The cost is what's different. The first step takes a refresh. The second takes a remodel that costs many times as much. Condition also changes how hard buyers compete: dated homes sold at a median 102% of their original list price, updated homes at 110%.
Two trust sales from this year. 35 French Creek Place, sold as-is with limited disclosures, listed at $2,400,000, sat 21 days and sold for $2,300,000. 1712 Lexington Avenue, also a trust sale, got fresh paint and new flooring, listed at $2,300,000 and sold for $2,620,000 in 9 days. The homes aren't identical (French Creek has the bigger lot, Lexington has a 601 sq ft enclosed patio room), but they're the clearest example this year of the two choices in front of you: what to fix first, and where to set the price.

Reading the numbers

Of the 29 Highlands single-family homes that sold with real market time in the last twelve months:

  • 23 homes (79%) priced to sell and sold over their original ask, a median of $2,920,000 (about 10% over asking), in a median of just 7 days.
  • 3 homes (10%) sold right at asking, in a median of 12 days.
  • 3 homes (10%) sold under their original ask. They started highest, a median list of $3,200,000, sold for a median of $3,000,000 (about 4% under), and took 18 days, more than twice as long.
  • Only one home in the whole year cut its price before selling: 2060 Bunker Hill Drive, a 1994 custom.
The difference is direction. The homes that priced to sell gained a median of $302,000 between list and sale. The three that listed above their tier lost a median of $107,500.
Why is the market pricing this way? Highlands buyers are chasing very few homes, so a list price at or a little under what the comps support pulls in a crowd and gets bid up, usually within a week or two. The three homes that sold under ask were the two most unusual in the neighborhood (a 2022 new build listed at $4,495,000 and a 1994 custom) plus one as-is trust sale, 35 French Creek Place, that listed above where its condition tier trades. Nothing in the last year rewarded pricing high and waiting.
What this all means. Price to the tier your home is actually in on listing day. As-is, that means pricing against French Creek, Brandywine and Forge, not against the refreshed homes. After a refresh, it means listing under the updated-tier sales and letting the competition carry it into range.

Special Considerations

No working heat is the one item that can affect a buyer's loan. FHA and VA loans require a permanent heat source, and conventional appraisers have to comment on heating and can make their value subject to repair. Most Highlands buyers use conventional, jumbo or cash, so it narrows the pool rather than blocking the sale, and the ranges in this report assume buyers price it in. If you'd rather solve it before listing, three to four mini-split zones run about $12,000 to $25,000 with the permit. Leave the radiant system in place and disclose it as not working; some Eichler buyers will want to restore it themselves.

The studio needs its paperwork. Pull the finaled permit and any plans before listing. A permitted, finished room gets real credit from buyers as an office or art studio, and an unpermitted one gets very little. None of the comps isolates what a studio like this adds, so it isn't priced as a separate line here, but it belongs at the top of the marketing.

Your lot is big, but Highlands prices don't show a lot premium. The largest-lot sales in this report sold in line with their condition tier, not above it: 1907 Ticonderoga (12,703 sq ft) and 2272 Bunker Hill (11,960 sq ft) landed among the other remodels, and 35 French Creek's 10,689 sq ft lot didn't lift it above the dated homes. Market the lot for what it allows (gardens, room to expand, a future ADU subject to county rules) rather than counting on it to add a price premium.

The sewer lateral is a real plus. Have the permit or invoice for the replacement in the disclosure package. Buyers of 1950s homes ask, and a done lateral takes one common repair off their list.

Order pre-listing inspections. A home inspection, a pest report and a roof inspection let buyers bid on facts instead of guesses. With a house that's been vacant for a while, that matters more than usual.
