The Market Story

The best comparison is down your own street. 10161 Stern Avenue, a 5-bed, 3-bath home of 2,625 square feet built in 1996, sold on January 23 for $3,738,000. It listed at $3,888,000 and went under contract in 7 days. That home is nearly your twin in age and size.

The newer builds run higher. 10831 Johnson Avenue, completed in 2024, sold for $4,200,000 in March. 18631 Barnhart Avenue, also 2024, closed at $4,132,500 in May. The new construction on Tuggle Avenue sold for $4,050,000 and $4,242,250. A 1998 build sits between those and the 1950s homes: 10536 Davison Avenue (1959) closed at $3,400,000 and 1068 Johnson Avenue (1956) at $3,500,000.

Your lot is a modest 4,947 square feet, smaller than most of the older homes, which is part of why a 1998 home your size prices below the new construction. A buyer here is paying for the Cupertino schools, the location near Apple Park and the finished size, not for land.

Across Cupertino and West San Jose, the typical comparable home went under contract in about 10 days and sold for roughly 104 percent of its original list price. There is about 1.2 months of inventory, far under the three months that marks a balanced market. That is a seller's market. The three-month median across the whole area was $2,467,500 in September, down from $2,800,000 in 2025 as the mix shifted, but buyers are still paying about 107 percent of the list price.

For you, that means steady competition for well-presented homes. Even the one home that struggled, 10536 Davison, needed four price cuts and 73 days, and still closed at $3,400,000. Pricing it right from day one is what sets the strong results apart.
