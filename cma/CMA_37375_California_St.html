The Market Story

Newark homes close to yours have been selling steadily. I went through the 3-bedroom sales within about a mile of you since April. The closest match to your layout is 37266 Spruce Street, a 3-bed, 1-bath, 1,048 square foot home from 1947. It sold on June 10 for $848,000 after a $68,000 first price cut and 54 days on the market.

Most of the other sales are 3-bed, 2-bath homes of 1,000 to 1,170 square feet, and almost all of them were updated. Updated and remodeled versions closed between $920,000 and $1,210,000. The top of that group, 37825 Goldenrod Drive, sold for $1,210,000 in June after a full renovation on a 7,200 square foot lot. On the quieter side, 5269 Abbey Court closed at $920,000 in September, and 36704 Burdick Street at $949,000 in July after one week on the market.

Your home is smaller than almost every sale on the list at 950 square feet, and it has one bath where most have two. That keeps its price below the middle of the pack, although the price per square foot runs higher for small homes. The numbers behind $865,000: the comparable homes sold for a median of $902 per square foot. Applied to your size that points to the low $850,000s, and a price in the mid $860,000s reflects the premium small homes carry per foot.

Across the market, the typical comparable home went under contract in about three weeks and sold for about 99 percent of its original list price. Newark has roughly two months of inventory, below the three months that marks a balanced market, so it leans toward sellers. The three-month median sale price across Newark and nearby Fremont was $1,400,000 in September, down from $1,650,000 at the 2025 peak, because the mix of homes selling changed. Buyers are paying about 100 to 101 percent of list, compared with 110 to 119 percent in early 2025.

For you, that means steady but less frantic demand. Well-presented homes still sell fast, and homes that start too high sit. 36563 Beutke Drive opened near $988,000, cut once, and took 109 days.
