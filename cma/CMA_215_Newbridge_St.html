The Market Story

Belle Haven has almost nothing for sale. As of September 29 there are 4 single-family homes on the market in the whole neighborhood, and 3 of them are already in contract. Over the last twelve months, 23 Belle Haven homes sold on the MLS at a median of $1,262,000. The median home sold in 10 days, for 107.4% of its original asking price.

The two updated sales closest to you had a bigger lot and a bigger house. 1140 Almanor Avenue, a block away, is a remodel of 970 square feet on a 6,874 square foot lot. It sold for $1,340,000 in August ($1,381 a square foot) after 94 days and a price cut. 1140 Berkeley Avenue, around the corner at 1,350 square feet, closed last week at $1,535,625. Most of the sales above $1.4 million nearby are remodels, flips or newer two-story homes of 1,200 to 1,800 square feet. The one smaller exception, 1125 Carlton Avenue, is a remodeled two-bedroom of 1,080 square feet with a designed Japanese garden, and it sold for $1,475,000 in June.

The houses that match yours most closely are the three-bedrooms of about the same size. The five updated ones sold for $1,190,000 to $1,262,000. 1117 Madera Avenue, exactly 1,040 square feet with a quartz kitchen, got $1,230,000 in 7 days. The two dated ones, with original or lightly refreshed kitchens and baths, sold for $1,130,000 and $1,150,000. Every one of them had a single bathroom.

Two nearby sales show how differently extra space can go. 1312 Carlton Avenue, an updated two-bedroom whose garage was also converted to a room with its own bath, listed at $1,185,000 as a two-bedroom with a bonus room and sold for $1,100,000 after 26 days. 1120 Henderson Avenue went the other way: county records show a two-bedroom of 1,070 square feet, it was marketed as a three-bedroom of 1,280, and it sold for $1,400,000 in 6 days.

The city line is worth real money. Across Willow Road in East Palo Alto, updated three-bedroom, two-bath homes of 1,110 and 1,150 square feet sold for $990,000 and $1,065,000 this year, and 1030 Bradley Way, a two-bedroom of the same 810 square feet as your county record, is still for sale after 81 days at $924,000. At the midpoint of the range below, your Menlo Park address is worth about $155,000 more than those East Palo Alto three-bedrooms.

How homes were priced mattered as much as what they were. Of the 15 updated and remodeled Belle Haven sales, the 10 that sold over their original list did it in a median of 10 days. The 4 that listed above their comps, 551 Newbridge, 1140 Almanor, 1127 Madera and 1312 Carlton, sat 26 to 94 days and sold under their original price. Two of those four had converted or in-law space, and buyers did not pay the asking premium for it.

Rates are the headwind. The 30-year fixed was 7.50% on September 28, up from 6.38% a year ago. On a $1,182,500 purchase with 20% down, that is about $710 a month more for the same house. Most of the sales in this report went into contract at lower rates. That is one reason the range below tops out at the middle of the updated three-bedrooms, not at their high.

Reading the numbers

Of the 23 Belle Haven homes that sold in the last twelve months:

  • 17 homes (74%) priced it to sell and sold over their original ask, a median of $1,275,000 (about 15% over), in a median of just 10 days.
  • 1 home (4%) sold at asking: 781 Ivy Drive, $1,200,000 on a $1,198,000 list, in 7 days.
  • 5 homes (22%) overpriced and sold below their original ask. They started highest (median list $1,398,000) but sold for a median $1,250,000, about 7% under, and took a median 42 days, four times as long.
  • 3 homes (13%) cut their price at least once before selling.
The overpriced group listed $298,100 higher and sold for $25,000 less. The first list price decided where each one ended up.
Why is the market pricing this way? The five that sold under ask priced off the wrong homes. 551 Newbridge listed at $1,650,000 after 1117 Henderson's full remodel sold for $1,626,000. 1140 Almanor listed at $1,398,000 for 970 square feet. 1127 Madera and 1312 Carlton asked buyers to pay for in-law and garage-conversion space, and buyers wouldn't. Meanwhile, buyers bid up 10 of the 13 homes listed between $998,000 and $1,200,000 within two weeks. With so little for sale, they compete hard for homes priced within reach and pass on the rest.
What this all means. Belle Haven buyers passed on list prices that reached for the remodels and bid up the ones that sat a little under the comps. For your home, that means marketing it as the 3 bed, 2 bath it lives like, disclosing the conversion up front, and listing below the updated three-bedrooms buyers will compare it with. The mid-tier band below, $1,050,000 to $1,125,000, asks buyers to compete 5% to 13% over list to reach the expected midpoint, less than the 15% the typical winner paid here in the last twelve months.

That's what I expect your home to sell for, not what I'd put on the sign. In Belle Haven those are two different numbers, and the gap between them is the strategy.

How the expected sales price was built. Size explains very little of price in Belle Haven's updated tier (r² of 0.08 across all 13 sales, 0.21 leaving out the home with an in-law unit), so this range comes from bracketing rather than a price per square foot. As a 3 bed, 2 bath of about 1,040 square feet, your home competes with the three-bedrooms of 910 to 1,140 square feet. If buyers discount the converted room because it isn't permitted, you sell like the two dated ones, $1,130,000 and $1,150,000, and their $1,140,000 midpoint sets the bottom of the range. If they take it as the third bedroom and second bath it is, your condition puts you with the five updated ones, $1,190,000 to $1,262,000, and their $1,225,000 median sets the top. The top stops at that median rather than their high because rates are about a point above where most of them went into contract, and because an appraiser will likely count only the county's 810 square feet. I left 1140 Almanor, a remodel on a 6,874 square foot lot that took 94 days, out of the bracket. A size fit across the updated tier, a cross-check only, puts 810 square feet at about $1,145,000 and a fully permitted 1,040 at about $1,264,000. With 230 of your 1,040 square feet unpermitted, your home belongs between the two. Your second bath is the one thing none of those three-bedrooms had.

Three ways to price into that expected sale, all marketed as a 3 bed, 2 bath with the conversion disclosed.

Aggressive list strategy
$1,025,000 to $1,049,000
Prices well under value to start a bidding war, $141,000 or more below what the updated three-bedrooms sold for. At the typical winner's 114.9% of list, it lands at about $1,178,000 to $1,206,000, around the middle of the expected range.

Pros: Draws the widest buyer pool, highest odds of multiple offers, typically the fastest sale.
Cons: If it does not draw competition, the sale can land at or near the low list price, which reads publicly as underperforming.
Mid-tier pricing strategy
$1,050,000 to $1,125,000
Lists under the expected price, and below the $1,190,000 to $1,262,000 updated three-bedrooms a buyer will compare it with. Reaching the expected midpoint takes a 5% to 13% jump, less than the 15% the typical Belle Haven winner paid.

Pros: Balances speed and upside, matches what most of the current market is actually doing.
Cons: Less dramatic than an aggressive list, so somewhat lower odds of a bidding-war ceiling.
Higher-end pricing strategy
$1,149,000 to $1,225,000
Lists inside the expected range, up to its $1,225,000 top. The higher the list, the more it asks buyers to pay for the converted garage before they compete, and the more it looks like the five Belle Haven homes that sold under ask: a median 42 days on market and a finish about 7% under the original price.

Pros: Keeps upside optionality if a premium buyer appears without a bidding war.
Cons: Longer time on market, a weaker hand in negotiation, usually requires one or more price cuts to reach the Expected Sales Price anyway.

Special Considerations

The converted garage. Your measurements put it at about 230 square feet: a room of about 10'5" by 17'9" plus its own toilet and shower. It has been part of the house for decades (MLS listings back to 1998 show it), and it was never permitted. The plan is to market it as the third bedroom and second bath and to say plainly in the listing and the disclosures that the conversion isn't permitted, so no buyer is surprised in escrow. The range above prices it that way: as a bedroom and bath buyers will use, discounted for the missing permit. As permitted living space, the house would trade with the updated three-bedrooms, $1,225,000 to $1,262,000, so the permit is worth roughly $40,000 to $80,000 over the midpoint. Legalizing it takes months of design, permits and inspections, which makes it a disclosure item for this sale rather than a pre-listing project. Belle Haven buyers are used to it: 1312 Carlton, 1151 Hollyburne and 1268 Carlton all sold in the last twelve months with converted or bonus space outside their permitted square footage.

The appraisal. An appraiser will likely count only the county's 810 square feet as living area and give the converted room less weight than the rest of the house. At the $1,182,500 midpoint that is $1,460 a square foot of the county's 810, above 1140 Almanor's $1,381, the highest among the updated sales. We're hoping the extra space from the converted garage helps, even though it isn't permitted, but that's not a guarantee. A buyer with a loan may need to cover a gap between the appraisal and the price. Offers with appraisal-gap coverage or large down payments are worth more to you than their headline number, and we'll weigh them that way.

The front yard. The large, well-designed front yard is a real selling point, and the backyard is small. 1125 Carlton marketed its designed garden front and center and sold for $1,475,000, 123% of its list price. Your front yard belongs in the first photos and the first line of the listing, because it is the outdoor space buyers will actually use.

Menlo Park, not East Palo Alto. Your home is inside Menlo Park's city limits, and the listing, the sign and every ad should say so. Updated three-bedroom, two-bath homes across Willow Road sold for $990,000 and $1,065,000 this year. The Menlo Park address is a large part of the difference.

The new roof. The 2024 reroof, from shake to composition shingle, is permitted and finaled with the city. It answers the first question buyers ask about a 1949 house, so it goes in the listing.
