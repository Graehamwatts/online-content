I want to be straight with you about this rather than only show you the numbers that help the case. The overall San Francisco condo market, and even the $3M-plus luxury tier specifically, is genuinely strong right now, prices up double digits year over year citywide, inventory down sharply, and luxury condo sales roughly quadrupling from a year ago. If I only showed you that, it would look like your unit should be appreciating, not sitting below your purchase price.

But that citywide luxury recovery is concentrated in a different product type than yours: newer, amenitized high-rises with modern systems, gyms, and no deferred maintenance, exactly the buildings that make up most of that 24-sale March count. Your own building's actual sales, and the eight other pre-war Pacific Heights comps above, tell a different story: a real, measurable gap that has not closed even as the broader luxury segment has taken off. 1388 Gough, a 1992 penthouse with a $3,700 monthly HOA, is sitting at 79 days after a price cut right now, in the same strong market. That is direct, current evidence that older product with high or rising HOA costs is not participating in the citywide recovery the same way newer buildings are, and it is the reason I'm not adjusting your number upward just because the overall market headlines look good.

Two Building-Specific Things Worth Knowing

The HOA at 1896 Pacific has climbed from $1,745-$1,860 a month in 2021 to $2,656 a month by the #202 sale in October 2025, a 52% increase in four years. That is a real cost that shows up in what buyers are willing to pay. Based on what I've been able to find, there also appears to be additional seismic retrofit work the building may still need, which typically goes hand in hand with an HOA increase of this size in a pre-war San Francisco building, special assessments and reserve funding for that kind of work are usually the driver. I have not built a specific dollar deduction for this into the ranges below, but it is a real part of why the ranges sit where they do rather than higher.
