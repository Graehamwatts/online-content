Four Offers Are Already In

I spoke with the listing agent directly. The seller has received four offers, some close to his number, and he's holding out for $2,050,000, above the $1,938,000 list price.

That's why it's sat 48 days with no price cut. It's not a lack of interest, it's a seller waiting for his number instead of taking what's already on the table.

$2,050,000 works out to $1,050 a square foot, above even this pull's full-remodel average of $1,038/sqft, on a home that's updated, not fully remodeled. The comps don't support that number. It may still be what it takes to win this specific house.

Where the Milpitas Market Is Right Now

Pulling every closed single-family sale in Milpitas between 1,600 and 2,300 square feet over the last six months gives a clean, size-matched read on this pocket: 32 closed sales, ranging from $1,280,000 to $2,400,000. Condition drives that spread far more than square footage does. A 2,001 sqft fully remodeled home on S Park Victoria Drive sold for $2,050,000 ($1,025/sqft) in 4 days. A 2,284 sqft dated home on Smith Lane sat 114 days and closed at $1,425,000 ($624/sqft). Same city, similar size, a $625,000 gap driven almost entirely by finish level.

Sorting those 32 sales by finish level puts your subject in context. A genuine full remodel, think designer kitchens, spa bathrooms, pools, tends to clear around $1,000 to $1,090 per square foot in this cohort. A clean, tastefully updated home like 2314 Farmcrest, granite counters, stainless appliances, newer flooring, but no gut remodel and no pool, clears closer to $850 to $980 per square foot. A dated or fixer home clears well under $800. At $992/sqft on the list price, 2314 Farmcrest is priced like it belongs in the full-remodel tier. The listing description does not describe a full remodel: no permitted addition, no pool, no wine cellar, no spa-inspired bath. It describes an updated home.

The single most useful comp in this entire pull is on the same street: 2291 Farmcrest Street, built the same era (1990 versus 1989), sold this window for $1,800,000 on 1,987 sqft, or $906/sqft, in 12 days. That is not a hypothetical adjustment, that is a real closed sale one block away.

  • The 18 active and pending listings competing for buyers right now (excluding two clear stale outliers) sit at a median of about 15 days so far.

The market is not slow right now, and neither is this listing behind the scenes. Correctly priced Milpitas homes in this range are selling fast and often over ask, and per the listing agent, this one has already drawn four offers, it just hasn't drawn one the seller will accept yet. The 48-day clock measures the seller's patience, not a lack of buyer interest.

Three Paths Forward. Offer Strategy

There is a real bidding war here: four offers in, seller holding at $2,050,000. Three ways to play it, from most conservative to most aggressive:

Path A. Hold at what the comps actually support.

Offer range: $1,850,000 to $1,900,000. This is the real, defensible ceiling backed by the updated-tier comps and 2291 Farmcrest specifically, with some room added since real competing offers exist. It is a genuine, walk-away-able number: if the seller won't move here, the comps say you are not overpaying by declining to chase.

Risk: with four offers already in and some reportedly close to the seller's number, this may simply not win. Use this if you are prepared to lose the house rather than pay above what the data supports.

Path B. Compete seriously without conceding the top. (recommended if you want a real shot)

Offer range: $1,980,000 to $2,020,000. This is close to where the other offers are reportedly clustering, without handing the seller his exact number on the first move. It puts you in the real competition for this specific house while leaving a small amount of room.

This is the recommendation if you want a genuine chance at this property. It acknowledges the real competitive situation the listing agent described, rather than pricing as if you are the only offer on the table.

Path C. Meet the seller's number.

Offer price: $2,050,000 or slightly above. This is what the listing agent says it actually takes right now. Go in clear-eyed: that is $1,050/sqft, above even this pull's full-remodel tier average of $1,038/sqft, for a home that is updated, not fully remodeled. You would be paying a real premium to win this specific house, not a number the comps independently justify.

Worth it only if you have decided this home, this street, this floor plan is worth paying above market to secure, not because the data says it is worth that much.

Special Considerations

HOA financial health, unverified

The $207/month HOA covers real value (pool, spa, clubhouse, exterior maintenance), but its reserve fund and any pending special assessments haven't been reviewed yet. Request the CC&Rs, reserve study, and the last 12 months of board minutes as part of your contingency period.

Owned solar, verify the paperwork

The listing states the Tesla solar system is owned outright, not leased, which is a real value-add if confirmed. Get the permit and interconnection documents during inspection; a truly owned system with no lien is worth asking about, but don't take it as settled until you've seen the paperwork.

Vacant property, utilities have been off

The listing notes water was shut off for showings. Have your inspector confirm plumbing, HVAC and irrigation function normally once utilities are restored; a home that's sat vacant for 48 days can develop minor issues (seals, appliances) that a lived-in home wouldn't show.

Three bedrooms in a four-bedroom market

Most of the competitive product in this size range in Milpitas is 4-bedroom. For your family, with a baby on the way, a 3-bedroom PUD may be exactly the right size and easier to maintain. Worth factoring into your own resale thinking down the line, not just your move-in plan.

Closing

2314 Farmcrest is a genuinely nice, updated home in a well-located Milpitas community with real schools, real HOA amenities, and an easy commute setup. It's priced about where a full remodel would clear, not where its own updated-but-not-remodeled condition actually supports on paper, and the listing agent says the seller already has four offers and wants $2,050,000 to let it go. Decide first how much you want this specific house versus staying disciplined to what the comps actually support, then pick the path above that matches that answer. Either way, point to 2291 Farmcrest by name, it is the closest thing to an apples-to-apples answer you have.
