3 Shorebreeze is a turnkey four-bedroom on a quiet corner of a cul-de-sac court, built in 2003 with its own solar. At 23 years old it is newer than the bulk of East Palo Alto's housing stock, most of which dates to the 1950s. Where the Market Is. And What That Means for You

East Palo Alto's single-family market has firmed up over the last year. The average sale price across the city's SFR cohort has climbed steadily through the first four months of 2026, from roughly $1.03M in January to roughly $1.23M in April, and homes are clearing at or above their asking price almost every month. You are bringing 3 Shorebreeze to market in late spring, which is consistently the strongest demand window of the year on the Peninsula. The timing is in your favor.

The number that matters most for you is where turnkey four-bedroom homes in your size band have actually been clearing. Over the last six months, the cluster of East Palo Alto homes most like yours (roughly 1,800 to 2,000 sq ft, newer or mid-vintage, four bedrooms) has sold between $1.31M and $1.48M, at $667 to $809 per square foot. Your independent automated valuation came in at $1,332,900, right in the middle of that band. The comps and the AVM are telling the same story.

The competition picture helps you too. Right now there is very little fresh, well-priced inventory in your exact lane. The two active four-bedroom homes priced near your range, 950 Mouton Circle at $1,549,000 and 1120 Alberni at $1,425,000, have been sitting for 62 and 117 days respectively, which tells you the market punishes an ambitious price. Below you, there is a wall of smaller three-bedroom, one-bath homes from $898K to $1.15M. Your home is materially better than that group, so it should not be priced into it.

Mortgage rates sit around 6.5% today, down from the 7%-plus peaks of 2023 but still well above the lows that drove the 2021 to 2022 run-up. That has thinned the buyer pool at the very top of the market, but it has not stopped well-presented, fairly priced East Palo Alto homes from selling quickly and over asking. The plan for 3 Shorebreeze is simple: price it where the comps say it clears, lean on the spring demand, and let the thin competition work for you.

Three Paths Forward. The Real Trade-Off

The question is not only what 3 Shorebreeze sells for, it is what you want this asset and the cash from it to do next. Three honest paths:

Path A. Sell now, take the cash, redeploy (recommended if your goal is to exit cleanly)

Realistic clearing range: $1,310,000 to $1,388,000. The home is vacant, turnkey, and you are sitting in the strongest selling season of the year with very little direct competition. List in the competitive band, run a standard two-to-three week marketing window, and let the spring demand and the thin inventory in your lane drive a fast, at-or-over-asking sale. This is the lowest-friction outcome and the one the data most supports right now.

Path B. Optimize for the top of the range. Light prep, then list.

Realistic clearing range: $1,375,000 to $1,450,000. The gap between your home and the $1.475M Wilks and Oakes sales is finish, not floor plan. A modest, targeted prep budget can close part of that gap. The highest-return items are replacing the few broken or dated windows, fresh neutral paint throughout, a deep clean, and light staging in the main living spaces and primary suite to help buyers see past the small front bedrooms. Because the home is vacant and already in late spring, you can do this prep inside two to three weeks without losing the season. The trade is a few thousand dollars and a short delay for a credible shot at the upper band.

Path C. Hold and rent. For a long-term real estate holder only.

This path fits one profile: you want East Palo Alto real estate as a multi-year hold, not a wait-and-see bet. A turnkey 1,840 sq ft four-bedroom in this area rents in the range of roughly $4,500 to $5,500 a month. Whether that covers your carrying cost depends entirely on the loan you put in place with the October 2024 refinance at $1,002,050. Pull your current loan statement and run the real monthly number against that rent range before considering this path. If the financing is favorable, a long hold with continued appreciation and principal paydown is defensible. If the refinance carries a market-rate payment, the property likely runs at a monthly loss, and the sell-and-redeploy path is the stronger use of the equity.

Notes & Caveats
Resale-restriction due diligence (do this before listing). 3 Shorebreeze was originally built in 2003 as a Below-Market-Rate (BMR) home, sold new for $189,500 with a $30,300 City of East Palo Alto assistance loan. The property then went through foreclosure in 2011 (taken by Post Apple LLC at a trustee's sale for $70,500), and you acquired it in 2012 for $465,000. In October 2024 it was refinanced for $1,002,050 on a conventional loan. Two facts strongly indicate the home is now market-rate, not price-restricted: a foreclosure typically extinguishes a subordinate City BMR resale covenant, and a $1M conventional refinance generally cannot close on a price-restricted property. The tax record still carries a legacy land-use tag ("Single Family Home, Bmr-101r"), which appears to be a leftover label rather than a live restriction. Before we list at market, confirm in writing with title and with the City of East Palo Alto's housing division that no resale restriction survives. This is the one item that must be cleared first. Everything in this analysis assumes it is.

Natural hazard zones

The prior listing flagged the property as being in a flood zone and a fault zone. These are common in parts of East Palo Alto and will be covered by the standard Natural Hazard Disclosure report, but buyers will see them and they can affect insurance cost and the buyer pool slightly. Worth ordering the NHD report early so it is ready for disclosure.

Condition and the path to the upper range

The home reads as turnkey but worn, with a few broken or dated windows and two small front bedrooms. The Wilks and Oakes comps that cleared near $1.475M were fully remodeled showpieces. The realistic, no-prep range for 3 Shorebreeze is the competitive band; the stretch band becomes credible only with the targeted window, paint, and staging work described in Path B.

Capital gains. Verify before you sell.

Your cost basis is roughly $465,000 from the 2012 purchase, so a sale near $1.35M implies a large taxable gain of around $885,000 before adjustments. The IRS Section 121 primary-residence exclusion ($250,000 single, $500,000 married) only applies if you have lived in the home as your primary residence for at least two of the last five years. Because the property is held in a trust and is currently vacant, that exclusion may not apply, and the gain could be fully taxable. Confirm your basis, holding structure, and any available exclusion or 1031 exchange options with your CPA before deciding to sell. This can materially change your real net.
