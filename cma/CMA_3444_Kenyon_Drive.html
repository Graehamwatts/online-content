The Market Story

Santa Clara's 95051 zip has moved a long way since you bought in 2015. This exact micro-pocket, the numbered-street and Kellogg/Kenyon/Cronin grid east of Lawrence Expressway, has been trading in the low-to-high $2 millions for homes your size and era over the past year, a market that would have felt unrecognizable a decade ago.

What's driving the spread: condition, almost entirely. I individually verified four recent sales in your immediate area, from a home directly across the street from you that sold with a light kitchen and bath update, up to a fully gut-remodeled home two blocks over that closed at nearly $1,800 a square foot. The gap between those isn't location, your whole pocket is walking distance to the same schools and the same Apple campus proximity that drove the frenzy in your own 2015 listing remarks. The gap is finish level.

The good news: buyers here are still paying over asking consistently, averaging 104 to 110 percent of list every month for the past year and a half, and the typical home in this zip is selling in three to four weeks. The market has cooled slightly from a February 2026 peak (109.5% of list) to a more recent 104-105%, but it hasn't softened in any way that should worry you. What matters more for your specific pricing decision is which comp set you're being measured against, and that's a conversation worth having before we set a number.
