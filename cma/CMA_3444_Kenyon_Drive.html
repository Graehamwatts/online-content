Reading the numbers. Of the 19 comparable Santa Clara homes that sold in the last six months:
  • 15 homes (79%) priced it to sell and sold over their original ask, a median of $2,450,000 (about 9% over asking), in a median of just 10 days.
  • Zero homes sold right at asking in this window, the market simply doesn't sit still at exactly 100%.
  • 4 homes (21%) overpriced and sold below their original ask. They started with a median list of $2,243,944 but sold for a median of $2,124,500, about 5% under, and took a median of 19 days, nearly double the priced-to-sell group.
  • Of the sales where I could confirm both the original and final list price, 1 of 7 (about 14%) needed at least one price cut before selling, 771 Pomeroy Avenue, which cut $75,000 and still closed under its reduced price.
Why is the market pricing this way? This looks like a straightforward anchoring gap. Most of the sellers in the "overpriced" bucket anchored to an optimistic number, a neighbor's headline sale, a Zestimate, the number they wanted to net, rather than what the actual comp set supported. The market corrected them over two to three weeks of soft activity, and they ultimately sold below where they started. The 79% majority who priced at or slightly under a defensible number got bid up fast instead. The correlation between price positioning and speed is real but modest (r = -0.23), the bigger reward for pricing right here isn't necessarily a faster sale, it's a better sale, the priced-to-sell group's median outcome is over $325,000 higher than the overpriced group's.

What this all means. The market punishes optimistic anchoring and rewards realistic, comp-backed pricing, not through speed alone but through final price. That directly supports the Competitive tier below: listing in a range the closest verified comps actually support puts you in the 79% majority that gets bid up, rather than risking the 21% minority that cuts and settles under ask.
