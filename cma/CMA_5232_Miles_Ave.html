The square footage discrepancy, stated plainly. Three numbers exist for this home. The county assessor's record: 1,303 sq ft (matches the home's last arm's-length sale in 2010). The MLS listing when it last sold in 2020: 1,618 sq ft. The current listing's claim: 2,665 sq ft. That means roughly 1,047 additional square feet, the entire finished lower level, has been added since 2020 and has never been reflected in the county's records. The listing agent's own private remarks state the current figure was measured by a photography company, not a licensed measurer, and is explicitly flagged as unverified. Before writing an offer, I would want: (1) confirmation of whether the lower-level finish was permitted, (2) an independent measurement (a licensed appraiser or a service like Certified Measurement), and (3) a review of the seller's disclosures for any mention of the work. This is common in older Oakland bungalows and is very fixable to understand, it just has to be checked, not assumed.

Do Not Offer Until Resolved
Verify first
Writing an offer before the sqft/permit question is answered means guessing which of the two scenarios above you're actually in, a $100,000-plus swing. Get the permit history and, ideally, an independent measurement before any number goes on paper.
How I'd sequence this: (1) I contact the listing agent this week with a specific question, not a generic one: is the lower level permitted, and how much of the claimed 2,665 sq ft is that space? At least one identical-vintage comp in this exact market (5347 James Ave) had a disclosed unpermitted bonus space, so this is a real, specific pattern to check for, not a shot in the dark. (2) If it is permitted, or the space is legitimately built out and disclosed the same way, Scenario A applies, and given the reconciled comp data I would recommend moving fast and closer to the top of that range. (3) If it is unpermitted or the usable space measures smaller, Scenario B applies, still a reasonable buy, just not the standout it first appears.
