Does having a tenant in place actually cost you money when you sell your San Mateo or Santa Clara County home? I already pulled each county's data separately, then combined both into one data set, 431 tenant-occupied closings against 9,590+ vacant closings over the last two years, to see if the pattern held at scale. It did, and it got stronger.

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
- Q: Does a tenant-occupied home sell for less than a vacant one across San Mateo and Santa Clara Counties?
  A: Yes. Over the trailing two years, single-family homes that sold with a tenant in place went for about 10.8% less per square foot than vacant homes ($1,002/sf tenant-occupied vs. $1,123/sf vacant, n=431 tenant-occupied vs. 9,590+ vacant).
- Q: Does a tenant in place mean the home sits on the market longer, combined across both counties?
  A: No, the opposite. Tenant-occupied homes had a median of 9 days to accepted offer vs. 11 days for vacant homes, about 2 days faster. List-to-sale ratios were close too (103.4% tenant-occupied vs. 105.7% vacant).
- Q: What should a San Mateo or Santa Clara County landlord do with this?
  A: Price to reflect the real per-square-foot gap, lock in tenant cooperation on showings in writing before listing, and don't assume waiting for vacancy buys you a faster sale, because across 431 real closings, it doesn't.
