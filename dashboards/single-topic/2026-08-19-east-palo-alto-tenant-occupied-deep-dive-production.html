I doubted my own East Palo Alto numbers, so I sent them back for a full recheck. Here's what a clean, non-overlapping sample of 54 tenant-occupied sales actually shows, and why my first read was wrong.

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
- Q: What went wrong with the first East Palo Alto data pull?
  A: The first read used two MLS pulls, a 5-year window and a 2-year window, treated as two independent confirmations. They weren't. The 2-year window sat entirely inside the 5-year window, so it was the same sales counted twice, not two separate checks.
- Q: What's the real East Palo Alto tenant-occupied price discount?
  A: Based on a corrected, non-overlapping sample of 54 tenant-occupied sales since 2020 (vs. 464 sales of everything else, owner-occupied and vacant combined), tenant-occupied homes in East Palo Alto sold for about 4.3% less per square foot ($760/sf vs. $794/sf). That's smaller than the county-wide 10-15% gap across San Mateo and Santa Clara Counties.
- Q: What about time on market and negotiating position?
  A: Tenant-occupied homes in East Palo Alto took about 25% longer to reach an accepted offer (15 days vs. 13), and closed about 2.6 points lower on the list-to-sale ratio (100.1% vs. 102.7%) than everything else, a real negotiating penalty.
- Q: What should an East Palo Alto landlord do with this?
  A: Don't assume the county's 10-15% price story applies locally. Budget for a modest time cost and some negotiating pressure instead, lock in tenant cooperation on showings before listing, and price with room to absorb list-to-sale pressure.
