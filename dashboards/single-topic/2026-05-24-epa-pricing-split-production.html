The 32-Day Home (What sells fast)
  • Priced at or below $/sqft median for condition tier
  • Move-in ready — no deferred maintenance flags
  • Professionally staged or furniture-enhanced
  • High-quality MLS photography
  • Sale-to-list ratio: at or above 100%
  • No price reductions
The 66-Day Home (What sits)
  • Priced 3–8% above $/sqft comp median
  • Deferred maintenance visible in listing photos
  • Vacant or lived-in — no staging
  • Average or below-average MLS photography
  • 70% eventually take a price reduction
  • Eventually sells for less than if priced right initially

"Homes that sold within 30 days were almost universally priced at or slightly below the price-per-sqft median for their condition tier. They came in at a number where a buyer could say 'this is fair' — and then offers stacked up.

[TEXT OVERLAY: "Overpriced by 3% = $33K gap on a $1.1M home"]
Homes that sat past 60 days were almost universally priced above that benchmark. Sometimes by 3%, sometimes by 8%. Doesn't sound like much. But in a market where buyers are running the math on a $5,500 monthly payment — $1.1 million, 20% down, at 6.37% — a 3% gap is $33,000. That's real money. And buyers walk.

[B-ROLL: Home with price reduction sign, days-on-market ticker graphic]
The other thing I found: the homes that sat past 60 days had a 70% price reduction rate. 7 out of 10 eventually dropped their price. The homes that sold in 30 days? Almost no reductions. They came in right and sold right."

[TRANSITION: cut]

[TALKING HEAD]
"So how do you know which group you'll land in before you list? Three variables.

[TEXT OVERLAY: "Variable 1: Price per sqft vs comp"]
First — price per sqft relative to comparable sold homes. If the last three comparable closings were at $650 per sqft and you're listing at $720, you're not the best deal on the market. You're the overpriced option. Buyers compare. They'll offer on the $650 house first.

[TEXT OVERLAY: "Variable 2: Move-in ready vs deferred maintenance"]
Second — condition. Move-in ready homes sell fast. Deferred maintenance gives buyers a reason to offer low or not at all. The math they do: if I'm spending $40K on a kitchen after closing, I'm offering $40K less than asking. Sometimes more.

[TEXT OVERLAY: "Variable 3: Staged vs vacant or lived-in"]
Third — staging. Vacant or lived-in homes consistently sit longer than staged homes. A staged home helps buyers see the size, the light, the flow. An empty home feels smaller. That hesitation adds weeks."

"If you're thinking about listing in the next 90 days, do this before you call any agent — including me.

Pull the last 6 months of closed sales on your street or immediate block. Calculate the median price per square foot for homes in similar condition to yours. Then honestly assess against those three variables: pricing, condition, staging.

Come in at or below that per-sqft benchmark, move-in ready, staged — you're in the 32-day group.

Price above it, deferred maintenance, no staging — you're likely in the 66-day group. And the 66-day group eventually takes less money anyway. They just wait longer to get there.
