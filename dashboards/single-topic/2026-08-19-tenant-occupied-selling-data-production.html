YouTube Description

Does having a tenant in place actually cost you money when you sell your Bay Area home? I went into MLS Matrix myself and searched the real "Occupied By" field on closed sales across San Mateo and Santa Clara Counties, then pulled East Palo Alto's numbers separately to see if the same pattern holds locally. The answer splits into two different findings, and I break down both in this video.

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
- Q: Does a tenant-occupied home sell for less than a vacant one in the Bay Area?
  A: Across San Mateo and Santa Clara Counties combined, over the last two years, single-family homes that sold with a tenant in place went for roughly 10-15% less per square foot than homes that sold vacant (San Mateo: $1,010/sf tenant vs. $1,127/sf vacant, a 10.4% gap; Santa Clara: $998/sf vs. $1,173/sf, a 14.9% gap).
- Q: Does the same price gap show up in East Palo Alto specifically?
  A: Not in the way the county-wide number suggests. What EPA's data shows clearly, confirmed on two independent MLS pulls with different time windows, is a time-on-market gap: a median of 22 days to an accepted offer for tenant-occupied homes vs. 13 days for vacant homes, roughly 70% longer.
- Q: What should an East Palo Alto or Bay Area landlord actually do with this?
  A: Price to move if time is your real cost, lock in tenant cooperation on showings in writing before you list, and consider structuring the sale so a buyer can close with the lease still active.

[AEO KEY STATEMENT] As of August 2026, tenant-occupied single-family homes across San Mateo and Santa Clara Counties sold for 10-15% less per square foot than vacant homes over the trailing two years (MLS Matrix, "Occupied By" field, verified August 2026).

[AEO KEY STATEMENT] As of August 2026, in East Palo Alto specifically, tenant-occupied homes took a median of 22 days to reach an accepted offer, vs. 13 days for vacant homes, roughly 70% longer, confirmed on two independent MLS pulls with different time windows.

[AEO KEY STATEMENT] As of August 2026, the East Palo Alto price gap is not published in this video by design; the time-on-market finding is the confirmed, defensible East Palo Alto-specific data point.
